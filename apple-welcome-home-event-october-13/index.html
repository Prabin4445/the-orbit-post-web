    Apple Sets Oct 13 "Welcome Home" Event for First Smart Home Hub

    Apple presented its HomeView smart home hub, a HomePod mini 2 and an updated Apple TV 4K at the Tuesday event in Tribeca. The smaller devices are reported to launch October 30, with the hub in mid November.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 11, 2026 · 14:30 UTC · Updated October 13, 2026 14:35 UTC5 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Updated 14:35 UTC: Apple used the Tuesday morning event in Tribeca to present the home lineup the preview coverage had pointed to.
    • Apple finally named the date for its smart home push.
    • The confirmation came Oct. 8 from Greg Joswiak, Apple's senior vice president of worldwide marketing, in a post on X.

    Updated 14:35 UTC: Apple used the Tuesday morning event in Tribeca to present the home lineup the preview coverage had pointed to. MacRumors reported the products as the HomeView smart home hub, a HomePod mini 2 and an updated Apple TV 4K, with invited reporters getting hands-on time from 9 a.m. Eastern. The HomeView pairs a roughly 6 inch square display with an A18 chip for Apple Intelligence and a new home operating system. It comes as a tabletop model with a metal speaker base in Gray, Silver, Starlight and Rose, and as a wall mounted version in Gray and Silver. The HomePod mini 2 and the updated Apple TV 4K are reported to go on sale on Friday, October 30, with the HomeView following in mid November. Reports at the time of this update had not shared a HomeView price. The preview below, published before the event, is kept for the record.

    Apple finally named the date for its smart home push. The company will hold a "Welcome home" event on Tuesday, Oct. 13, at 9 a.m. Eastern in Tribeca, New York, where reporters expect the first details of a long-rumored home hub often called the HomePad.

    Advertisement

    The confirmation came Oct. 8 from Greg Joswiak, Apple's senior vice president of worldwide marketing, in a post on X. Media invites followed for what Apple calls an "Apple Experience," a hands-on format it reserves for products reporters are meant to touch.

    Beyond that invitation, almost nothing is confirmed. Apple has said nothing about specific products. Everything that follows comes from reporting, not from Apple, and the record on Apple hardware rumors is mixed. Read each detail accordingly.

    What the reports say

    Reporting from MacRumors, 9to5Mac and Bloomberg's Mark Gurman converges on a first-ever smart home hub, developed under the code name J490. The device is said to pair a roughly 6-inch square display with a speaker base, a design some describe as an iPad meeting a HomePod. Siri voice control, powered by the smarter AI version of Apple's assistant, would run the show.

    Reports also describe a camera and presence sensor that could tune the screen's layout to whoever is in the room, and two versions: one that mounts on a wall and one that sits on a tabletop half-dome base. A refreshed HomePod mini and a new Apple TV 4K are also said to be on the bill. A second October event, around Oct. 27, is expected to cover Macs and iPads, per Bloomberg.

    One figure making the rounds is a price near $350. It is unconfirmed, and Apple has given no hint of pricing.

    Why it matters

    Apple is years late to the smart display. Amazon's Echo Show has sold in the millions since 2017, and Google's Nest Hub owns the kitchen counter in plenty of homes. Apple's HomePod mini is a fine speaker, but the company has never shipped a screen for the home.

    The reason, per years of reporting, is Siri. The hub was reportedly held back until the AI overhaul of Siri was ready. That wait had a cost. Every year without a screen ceded routines, video calls and kitchen timers to rivals, and every one of those habits made the next Apple sale harder. Tuesday is the moment Apple starts climbing back, if the product is as far along as the rumors suggest.

    The context

    There is also a hardware partnership angle. Reports have linked LG to Apple's home ambitions, with a doorbell, a thermostat and cameras among the possible projects. A hub alone does not make a smart home. The ecosystem around it decides whether the device becomes a daily habit or a gadget that gathers dust.

    Watch the Home app. Apple has spent years courting the Matter standard, which lets devices from different brands work together. If the hub becomes the best Matter controller in the house, Apple does not need to build every sensor itself. It just needs to be the brain people trust.

    The privacy pitch

    Here is where Apple has an opening its rivals gave it. Amazon and Google built the smart home category on always-listening microphones and advertising businesses that feed on user data. Both companies have faced scandals and lawsuits over human reviewers hearing private recordings and over vague data-retention policies.

    Apple, which sells hardware and services rather than ads, can credibly promise that the camera and presence sensor in your kitchen never send raw footage to a server. If the hub processes requests on-device and keeps personal layouts local, privacy becomes the feature. That pitch only works if the behavior is real, and the first teardown reviews will test it. But it is the one argument neither Amazon nor Google can make with a straight face.

    What happens next

    Tuesday's event is expected to combine an online video with hands-on time in New York. Expect pricing, ship dates and the Siri demos Apple has been withholding. If the hub ships this fall, the real test comes after the holidays, when the novelty fades and the device either earns a permanent spot on the counter or becomes an expensive clock.

    The second event, around Oct. 27, should bring the Mac and iPad updates, per Bloomberg. For now, all eyes are on whether "Welcome home" is a slogan or a product line.

    **Key numbers**

    • Oct. 13, 9 a.m. ET: the event, in Tribeca, New York
    • ~6 inches: reported size of the hub's square display
    • 2 form factors reported: wall-mount and tabletop half-dome base
    • ~$350: rumored price, unconfirmed
    • ~Oct. 27: expected second October event for Macs and iPads
