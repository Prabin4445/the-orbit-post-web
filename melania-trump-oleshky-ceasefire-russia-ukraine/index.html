    Melania Trump Secures Localized Ceasefire for Oleshky Between Russia and Ukraine

    The White House said Russia and Ukraine agreed to stop all military action in the front-line city of Oleshky from October 29, so aid can enter and residents who want to leave can evacuate through a designated safe passageway.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 23, 2026 · 13:55 UTC · Updated October 23, 2026 19:47 UTC4 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Updated 19:47 UTC, Oct. 23, 2026.
    • Both Russian and Ukrainian officials confirmed the limited ceasefire on Friday, CNN reported.
    • First Lady Melania Trump has secured a localized ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine for the city of Oleshky, the White House said on Friday, October 23.

    Update: Russia gives an Oct 27 start, two days before the Oct 29 date

    Updated 19:47 UTC, Oct. 23, 2026. Russia's Ministry of Defense said on Friday it would implement the ceasefire in the Oleshky region beginning at 4 a.m. Moscow time on October 27, CNN reported. Russia's top military officer, General Valery Gerasimov, also gave October 27 as the start in comments to state media, USA Today reported. The first lady's office has given October 29 as the start date, and it is not yet clear which date the guns will follow.

    Both Russian and Ukrainian officials confirmed the limited ceasefire on Friday, CNN reported. Ukrainian authorities said around 1,700 civilians remain in the city and that supply routes for food and medicine have been blocked. The city's pre war population was roughly 24,000. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov credited the first lady for her significant contribution and said President Vladimir Putin and President Donald Trump discussed the topic in a recent call. The first lady said non military civilian leaders from both countries will visit residents in their homes to assess what help they need, and that residents who want to leave will get transport to their preferred destination, in Russia or in Ukraine. Newsmax said its cameras will have exclusive access to document the effort. President Trump told reporters the two sides both wanted to do it and said of his wife, she has done a great job.

    Advertisement

    First Lady Melania Trump has secured a localized ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine for the city of Oleshky, the White House said on Friday, October 23. Both countries agreed to temporarily stop all military action in the city to allow humanitarian aid to enter and to prepare a civilian evacuation through a designated safe passageway. The localized ceasefire will begin on October 29, 2026.

    Reuters reported the White House announcement on Friday, and Sky News carried the same statement from the Office of the First Lady. The agreement covers one city only. It is not a general ceasefire in the war.

    "The situation in Oleshky is dire. Oleshky is a front-line war city on the brink of disaster," the first lady said in the statement. She said the remaining residents are mainly elderly people who urgently need medical attention, medicine, food and clean water, and that people there have limited electricity and endure regular military attacks amid mined roads.

    What the agreement covers

    Under the terms announced on Friday, non-military, civilian leaders from both countries will work together to visit Oleshky residents in their homes and determine the level of assistance each household requires. Residents who wish to evacuate will receive coordinated support and transportation to their preferred destination. The first lady said she anticipates some residents will want to go to Russia and some to Ukraine.

    She also said an impartial American media organization will accompany and document the Russian and Ukrainian teams on the ground, to help ensure the wishes of each family are respected.

    Both nations assigned teams to coordinate the mechanics of the pause, including demining, and the first lady said all parties have worked around the clock.

    A front-line city with about 2,000 people left

    Oleshky is located in southern Ukraine, across the Dnipro River from Kherson. Before the war, roughly 24,000 people lived in the city. The White House estimates that 1,700 to 2,000 residents remain today, mainly older people unable to leave, along with about 50 children.

    The city has remained on the front line since the war began, and much of its population has been displaced or evacuated. Following the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam, approximately 90 percent of the city's territory was flooded, the statement said.

    How the first lady got involved

    The White House said the situation in Oleshky came to the first lady's attention during a private discussion at her Fostering the Future Together event in New York City during the 81st United Nations General Assembly. She acted in a formal role the statement calls Exclusive Intermediary and worked with her representative directly with Russian and Ukrainian leadership to define the terms of the agreement.

    The statement said she and her representative used a communication channel with Russia and Ukraine that was established through the previous six reunifications of 34 children.

    The city drew wider international attention a week earlier. On October 16, 2026, Pope Leo phoned Russian President Vladimir Putin and asked him to agree to a ceasefire so aid and food could enter Oleshky, and to allow a voluntary evacuation. The Kremlin said Putin supported the Vatican intention to help protect the town population and organise vital supplies.

    The White House statement gave no end date for the localized ceasefire.
