    City Rout 10-Man Liverpool 3-0 in First Match Since Guilty Verdict

    Days after an independent commission found City guilty of 114 financial rule breaches, goals from Haaland, Doku and Cherki sealed a 3-0 win over 10-man Liverpool at Anfield amid boos, protest banners and a post-match police intervention.

    LIVERPOOL: Manchester City gave the loudest answer possible to their loudest critics on Sunday, routing Liverpool 3-0 at Anfield in their first match since an independent commission found the club guilty of 114 breaches of Premier League financial rules.

    The league leaders were greeted with boos and banners reading 'Cheats' and 'Guilty 114' from the home crowd. City answered on the pitch. Liverpool were reduced to ten men in the 62nd minute when Ryan Gravenberch earned a second booking for a foul on Jeremy Doku. Five minutes later, Erling Haaland smashed in the opener. Doku curled home the second, and Ryan Cherki capped the night with a spectacular solo goal for the third.

    Advertisement

    The win kept City's perfect league start intact: six straight victories from six games, and the club's winning run stretched further across all competitions.

    After the final whistle, police moved to separate the two sets of supporters. A small group of City fans were encircled by officers as tensions rose outside the away end. The Manchester Evening News reported one Liverpool supporter was pepper-sprayed and batons were drawn, though no major disorder followed.

    Why it matters

    The verdict, confirmed by the Premier League on September 29, is the biggest scandal in English football's modern era. The commission found City used sham sponsorship contracts to disguise more than 830 million pounds in owner-linked funding between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons, and said some club witnesses gave false or dishonest evidence.

    City have appealed, insisting they hold what they call irrefutable evidence of innocence. No punishment takes effect while the appeal runs. If the verdict stands after a separate sanctions process, the options range from fines and transfer bans to points deductions, retrospective title stripping, and even expulsion from the league.

    Money is already moving. Manchester United, Liverpool, Arsenal, and Tottenham have served notice of their right to seek compensation if the verdict holds; reports put hypothetical claims near 1 billion pounds. Bloomberg reported City held early settlement talks with the league before the verdict, but the discussions collapsed as City maintained its innocence.

    The context

    City manager Enzo Maresca refused to discuss sanctions before the trip to Anfield. His players made the other point for him. Haaland said after the match: "It's about these times, just stay strong mentally, stick together and do what you can do something about. What we can do something about is to play good football and to fight together because in the end, that's the only thing we can do." (Reuters, via USA Today)

    Liverpool's afternoon fell apart after Gravenberch's dismissal. The midfielder had carried the hosts' fight for an hour, but his red card stretched the game, and City's front three needed no second invitation.

    What's next

    The appeal now sits with a new independent panel, a process likely to run for months. City's immediate schedule offers no let-up: a home tie with Paris Saint-Germain follows the Anfield trip. On the pitch, the champions-elect keep winning. Off it, the league's biggest legal fight in memory is only getting started.

    Key numbers

    • 114 of 115: charges the commission found City guilty of
    • 830m to 900m pounds: owner-linked funding disguised through sham contracts, per the commission
    • 3-0: the final score at Anfield
    • 6: straight league wins to open City's season
