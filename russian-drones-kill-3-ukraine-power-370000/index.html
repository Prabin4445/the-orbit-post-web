    Russian Attacks Kill at Least Eight in Ukraine, Cut Power to a Million

    Ukrainian officials raised the toll from Friday's attacks across nine regions to at least eight killed and 60 injured. Power was cut to more than one million people, and crews later restored supply to more than a million.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 16, 2026 · 13:12 UTC · Updated October 17, 2026 12:30 UTC4 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Updated 12:30 UTC, Oct 17, 2026.
    • Updated 10:42 UTC, Oct 17, 2026.
    • Updated 06:28 UTC, Oct 17, 2026.

    Updated 12:30 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Ukraine's air force said Russia attacked overnight with 332 attack drones, four Kalibr cruise missiles, and ballistic and Kh-22/32 cruise missiles, and that air defences shot down or suppressed 293 targets, including all four Kalibr missiles and 289 drones. Impacts were recorded at 22 locations, with debris falling at three others. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said more than 15 critical power generation and transmission facilities were targeted, and he called the barrage one of Russia's largest attacks on Ukraine's energy sector. He said Lutsk, Rivne, Zhytomyr and Vinnytsia were among the worst hit areas. In Kherson, a Russian air strike on the Korabelniy district killed a 64 year old ambulance driver and injured 19 people, including a child, Governor Oleksandr Prokudin said. In Sloviansk, a strike on a residential area killed one person and injured 10, local authorities said. Grid operator Ukrenergo announced emergency power cuts in several regions.

    Updated 10:42 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Russia's defence ministry said on Saturday that its forces had carried out a large-scale overnight attack on Ukrainian infrastructure, hitting the country's largest steelworks in Kryvyi Rih, an electricity substation in Zhytomyr, another steel plant in Zaporizhzhia, and a cargo vessel in the Black Sea that Moscow alleged was delivering military cargo to the port of Odesa. The Kryvyi Rih plant is owned by ArcelorMittal, which suspended the facility's operations in September after a series of Russian ballistic missile strikes. Reuters reported that it could not independently confirm the ministry's account. This desk found no Ukrainian confirmation of those specific hits in the reports reviewed on Saturday morning.

    Updated 06:28 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. The toll from the Friday attacks has risen. Ukrainian officials said on Saturday, October 17 that Russian attacks across nine regions on Friday, October 16 killed at least eight people and injured 60, hit two shopping centres, and cut power to more than one million people. Ukraine's Ministry of Energy said repair crews had restored power to more than a million people who lost it on Friday.

    Two people were killed in Kramatorsk, a frontline city in the Donetsk region, and two more in the southeastern Dnipropetrovsk region. In Rivne, drones hit a major shopping centre and other sites, killing one person and wounding seven, Governor Oleksandr Koval said. In Volyn, drones also hit a customs terminal in the town of Kovel, on a major road from Kyiv to Poland, and set fire to about 10 trucks. A shopping centre in Sumy was also hit.

    Russia's military chief told President Vladimir Putin on Friday that the military had allocated the assets needed to press its aerial campaign against Ukraine's energy system, Al Jazeera reported.

    Russian drones killed at least three people across Ukraine early on Friday, October 16, and cut power to about 370,000 people in the northwest after strikes on the energy grid, officials said. The attacks hit homes, shops and a food plant in four regions, and they came while European leaders were gathering in Brussels to back Ukraine for another winter of war.

    Two people died and 12 were wounded in attacks on three districts of the eastern city of Kharkiv, Governor Oleh Syniehubov said. In Volyn region, which borders Poland, a drone strike on a food processing facility killed one person and wounded eight others, Governor Roman Romaniuk said.

    Power out in the northwest

    The strikes on the grid left about 370,000 people without electricity in Volyn, Romaniuk said. Repair crews had reconnected about 100,000 people by later in the morning. That still left about 270,000 people without power, a figure Swedish wire TT also reported on Friday morning.

    In Lutsk, the regional capital, Mayor Andriy Razumovskyi said the blackout could last for at least 24 hours. Poland scrambled fighter jets while the attacks were under way, the Polish military said. Polish officials later said no violation of the country's airspace had been recorded.

    Drones also hit at least two shopping centres. One was in Sumy, a northern city close to the front line. The other was in the neighbouring Rivne region, where Governor Oleksandr Koval said seven people were wounded. Emergency service pictures from Sumy showed the damaged entrance of the centre there.

    Brussels backs Kyiv as the strikes land

    The overnight attacks came one day after Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil refineries. Ukrainian drones hit the refinery at Ryazan on Thursday, October 15, after a three day pause. Kyiv said it stopped pausing because Russia had kept hitting Ukrainian power plants and other energy sites, despite an energy ceasefire announced by US President Donald Trump that neither side had confirmed in full.

    President Volodymyr Zelenskyy spent Friday, October 16 at a European Union summit in Brussels. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the bloc had supported Trump's energy ceasefire, but Russia had not respected it for a single minute and had escalated instead. She said the EU is preparing a 22nd package of sanctions on Russia, and that existing sanctions will not be lifted unless Russia stops the war.

    European Council President Antonio Costa said EU support for Ukraine remains strong, and leaders discussed winter help for Ukraine's energy system. That help now has a fresh test. Crews in Volyn are working to bring back power to hundreds of thousands of people before the next night of strikes.
