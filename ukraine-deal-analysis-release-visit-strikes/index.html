    Ukraine Peace Talks: A Release, a Visit and New Strikes in One Day

    No deal terms have been published. A freed American, a framework outline from Oslo, a dispute over Patriot missiles and fresh strikes on the power grid show movement around a deal, not a deal itself.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 21, 2026 · 14:30 UTC · Updated October 21, 2026 15:56 UTC5 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • **Updated 15:56 UTC: Developing story.**
    • Russia has agreed in principle to resume three way peace talks with Ukraine and the United States after November 10, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said after the St. Petersburg meeting ended.
    • Ushakov was cooler about the talks Witkoff held in Oslo a day earlier.

    **Updated 15:56 UTC: Developing story.**

    Russia has agreed in principle to resume three way peace talks with Ukraine and the United States after November 10, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said after the St. Petersburg meeting ended. Ushakov said the next round could take place in Abu Dhabi at a technical level and would focus on building a security system for Russia and Ukraine once the war ends, Reuters reported. President Vladimir Putin told United States envoy Steve Witkoff that Russia was ready to resume the talks at Witkoff's request, CNN reported. The meeting at the Constantine Palace lasted nearly three hours.

    Advertisement

    Ushakov was cooler about the talks Witkoff held in Oslo a day earlier. He told reporters that the new approaches Witkoff brought from Kyiv and its European partners contained little of actual value for resolving the crisis, CNN reported. Putin also raised restoring economic cooperation with the United States and a possible meeting with China to discuss Ukraine, CNN reported. There was no sign that Russia had dropped its earlier demands, which include Ukraine giving up the whole Donbas region, cutting its military sharply and pledging never to join NATO, CNN reported.

    No peace deal for Ukraine has been published, and no source has released any deal terms. What formed on Wednesday was a set of signals pointing in different directions at the same time. Russia freed an American prisoner at the start of talks in St. Petersburg. Ukraine pressed Washington over delayed air defense missiles. Russian strikes killed four people overnight and hit energy sites in four regions.

    The only framework described so far came from talks in Oslo on Tuesday. A United States official said Ukraine brought new ideas and the sides made meaningful progress on a peace framework. That framework, as described, covers long term security guarantees, a post war economic plan and a wider European security structure. Kyiv restated its red lines in Oslo: security guarantees and no surrender of territory. Beyond that outline, nothing has been agreed in public, and Kyiv has not been shown any final text about Ukraine itself.

    What happened

    President Vladimir Putin announced the release of Stephen Hubbard at the start of talks in St. Petersburg with United States envoy Steve Witkoff on Wednesday, October 21. Putin said Hubbard was at Pulkovo airport. Hubbard is 74. Russian authorities detained him in 2022. A closed trial convicted him of mercenary activity and he was serving six years and 10 months. His family and lawyer say the charges were false. Hubbard was the only American held by Russia whom Washington had designated as wrongfully detained. President Donald Trump said there was no swap.

    The St. Petersburg meeting was the ninth in person meeting between Witkoff and Putin. Jared Kushner joined remotely. At the same talks, Putin offered Russian help on Iran and large fertilizer supplies to the United States. He also raised a possible meeting in China next month with Trump and possibly Xi Jinping, according to Reuters. No readout had set out agreed Ukraine terms.

    Overnight, Russian strikes killed four people and hit energy facilities in the Kyiv, Volyn, Odesa and Mykolaiv regions, Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi said on Wednesday. Two of the dead were in Kyiv, where a drone hit an apartment building. The other two were an 83 year old man and an 81 year old woman outside Kharkiv.

    The background readers need

    Hubbard was detained in Izyum in February 2022, after Russia launched its full scale invasion. His case ran through a closed process, which his family and lawyer challenged from the start.

    The missile dispute is separate and older than this week. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told United States senators on Monday, October 19 that the Pentagon is delaying Patriot interceptor missiles already paid for by European countries. He said a package awaits the signature of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. He said European partners are ready to transfer interceptors from their own stocks but need United States approval. His words were direct: "The missiles are ready, and they could already be in Ukraine."

    United States officials rejected the claim that anything was deliberately denied. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Patriot interceptors are in short supply elsewhere. Zelenskyy also said intelligence early warnings had lapsed and Starshield satellite speeds were limited. After the Oslo talks, he said early warning intelligence sharing works again and thanked Washington, according to BBC reporting. Trump has pressed Zelenskyy to make a deal and suggested Kyiv might need a different negotiator.

    Why it matters

    The three signals pull against each other. A prisoner release can build trust for talks. A delay over interceptors leaves Ukrainian cities exposed while talks continue. Fresh strikes on energy sites raise the cost for civilians as winter approaches and Russia presses the power grid again.

    For ordinary people in Ukraine, the interceptor question is not abstract. It decides whether air defenses can protect apartment blocks and power plants on nights like the last one. For people in the United States, the war and fuel prices are live issues in the midterm campaign, 13 days out from October 21.

    The Oslo outline matters because it names what any settlement must settle. Security guarantees decide whether Ukraine believes a ceasefire would hold. A post war economic plan decides who pays to rebuild. A wider European security structure decides how neighbors live with the result. None has a published answer yet.

    Key numbers

    Hubbard is 74 and had been held since 2022. His sentence was six years and 10 months. Wednesday was the ninth in person Witkoff and Putin meeting. Four people were killed overnight, two in Kyiv and two outside Kharkiv. The dead outside Kharkiv were 83 and 81. Strikes hit energy facilities in four regions: Kyiv, Volyn, Odesa and Mykolaiv. United States midterm elections are 13 days away.

    What happens next

    Watch four things. First, read the Witkoff and Putin readout closely for any Ukraine terms, not only general language about progress. Second, watch whether interceptor approvals move and whether the package Zelenskyy described gets a signature. Third, watch whether Hubbard is a one off release or the first item in a wider package. Fourth, watch whether Kyiv sits at the table for any terms about Ukraine, and whether those terms respect the red lines Kyiv restated in Oslo.

    Until those answers appear, the honest description is narrow. Talks have a framework outline from Oslo. A prisoner is free. Missiles Ukraine says it needs have not arrived, by Kyiv account, and strikes continue. That is movement around a deal, not a deal itself.
