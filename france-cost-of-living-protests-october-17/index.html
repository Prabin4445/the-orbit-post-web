    France Sets Second Curfew Night, Vows Massive Police Deployment

    Paris, Bordeaux and Lille will run curfews again on Tuesday night, Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu promised a massive police deployment for Saturday protests, and the military will back up police after a weekend that saw more than 2,000 arrests and at least two deaths.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 17, 2026 · 11:54 UTC · Updated October 21, 2026 0:01 UTC16 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • France will run localized curfews for a second straight night on Tuesday, and the government has promised a massive police deployment for fresh protests called for Saturday.
    • Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu credited the targeted curfews with helping maintain calm overnight into Tuesday.
    • Interior Minister Laurent Nunez told MPs at the National Assembly that he deeply regretted the weekend violence and accepted some responsibility.

    Updated 00:01 UTC, Oct 21, 2026

    France will run localized curfews for a second straight night on Tuesday, and the government has promised a massive police deployment for fresh protests called for Saturday. Paris police set an overnight curfew from 11 p.m. Tuesday in the worst hit areas around Place de la Republique and Les Halles, and Bordeaux and Lille will also impose curfews, CNN reported. Paris also banned public gatherings on the Champs-Elysees and at train stations, and temporarily banned people from carrying cans or bottles of petrol or gas, AFP reported.

    Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu credited the targeted curfews with helping maintain calm overnight into Tuesday. He admitted that intelligence services had failed to identify the severity of the situation and said the previous security arrangements had been too static. "The deployment for next Saturday will be massive," Lecornu told lawmakers. The Sentinelle military force will be used mainly on static security missions, which the government says will free police officers and gendarmes for law enforcement work, AFP reported.

    Interior Minister Laurent Nunez told MPs at the National Assembly that he deeply regretted the weekend violence and accepted some responsibility. "I accept my share of the responsibility, but I would not want us to forget the significant responsibility of a certain political camp that does not hesitate to add fuel to the fire, nor the responsibility of the troublemakers," Nunez said.

    The student union UNEF has called further protests for Saturday and said it will not give up until its demands are met, and the CGT union federation said it will join, calling for peaceful protests. Curfews followed a weekend in which more than 2,000 people were arrested, with at least two deaths and serious injuries that included two people who lost their hands, CNN reported.

    Updated 10:19 UTC, Oct 20, 2026. The first localized curfews took effect on the night of Oct 19, Reuters, Le Monde and AFP via Brussels Signal reported. In central Paris, a curfew ran around Place de la Republique and Les Halles from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m., with gatherings banned in designated areas from 8 p.m. Curfews also took effect in Lyon. In Lille, Douai, Wattignies and Denain, a curfew for minors ran from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. on Monday and Tuesday nights. Restrictions in Bordeaux and Nice were set to run for several days, and Marseille had already banned gatherings. Police and gendarmerie sources reported a largely quiet night across the country, the first night without serious disorder since the Oct 17 rioting, with scattered incidents during demonstrations in Martinique. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said targeted curfews had contributed to the calm and that fresh curfews would be imposed on the night of Oct 20 wherever local prefects and mayors judged them necessary. Justice Minister Gerald Darmanin said 2,513 people had been taken into custody, 67 of them jailed, with dozens of judicial investigations opened. About 1,000 police officers were injured, and one demonstrator lost a hand to a crowd-control grenade, AFP figures showed. On turnout, authorities put Oct 17 participation at 550,000, while the CGT union put it at more than 800,000.

    Updated 14:21 UTC, Oct 19, 2026. Authorities estimated on Monday that at least 3,000 businesses were damaged or looted in the weekend violence, AP reported, a higher count than the roughly 1,500 shops the retail federation said were directly hit on Saturday. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said the damaged stores would be eligible for government help, including tax relief and unemployment funds, The Wall Street Journal reported. Lecornu told reporters he was fully resolved to restore public order and that a state of emergency could be declared if deemed necessary, but that partial curfews were more appropriate at this stage, Reuters reported. He ruled out a full curfew for all of Paris. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez, speaking on France Inter radio, said he had submitted his resignation because he is responsible for everything that is happening, and acknowledged that authorities had lost control in some major cities on Saturday, AP reported. Nunez said turnout was about twice what his ministry had anticipated and that police strategy needs to adapt to extremely mobile groups. He also told reporters there were no plans to cancel Tuesday evening's Paris Saint Germain match against Barcelona, Reuters reported.

    Updated 12:41 UTC, Oct 19, 2026. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said on Monday he had asked prefects to impose partial curfews in some cities from tonight, Reuters and The Guardian reported. He described the curfews as very limited and localized, said details would come from local authorities later on Monday, and blamed the ultra-left and criminal groups for exploiting the protests. The arrest count from the weekend has risen to 2,400, CNN and The Guardian reported. Lecornu said that despite the severe violence and property damage on Saturday, no one was killed that day.

    Updated 09:40 UTC, Oct 19, 2026. Student protesters blocked access to the Louvre Museum in Paris on Monday morning, France 24 and AFP reported. The students said they were acting in support of the high school movement and against police repression, Newsweek reported. In Marseille, public gatherings were banned from noon to 8 p.m. on Monday, Le Monde reported. French stocks fell in early trading as investors reacted to the weekend violence, with the CAC 40 index down 1.02 percent by 07:08 GMT, Reuters reported.

    Updated 05:43 UTC, Oct 19, 2026. President Emmanuel Macron has declined Interior Minister Laurent Nunez's offer to resign after the widespread weekend rioting, the Elysee presidential office said on Monday, Reuters reported. Macron said Nunez should stay on to keep a chain of command within the police and security forces. He also asked Nunez to set up new approaches to deal with looters as quickly as possible, the Elysee said.

    Updated 20:23 UTC, Oct 18, 2026. The Interior Ministry toll rose again late on Sunday and replaces the figures in the update below. The ministry put arrests at 2,397, with 997 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters injured, seven of them seriously, Reuters and AFP reported on Sunday evening. The update below carried the ministry's 6 p.m. figure of 2,247 arrests and 602 injured. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu's crisis meeting with ministers, first planned for Sunday evening, was moved to Monday. On deaths, the confirmed protest death is the man in his thirties who suffered a cardiorespiratory arrest in Paris on Saturday and died in hospital, according to the Paris police prefecture. An earlier version of this story also counted a 22-year-old police officer who died after being struck by a vehicle in Nantes, following CNN reporting that cited Interior Minister Laurent Nunez. French reports describe that incident as a separate intervention in the night of Friday to Saturday, before Saturday's protests, and do not describe it as a protest death, so it is no longer counted in this story's protest toll.

    Updated 19:30 UTC, Oct 18, 2026. President Emmanuel Macron addressed the nation on television on Sunday evening and condemned the looting and burning that followed Saturday's cost of living protests. 'Nothing justifies it, nothing explains it, nothing excuses it,' Macron said of the images from Saturday night, Reuters reported. He said those who destroyed, burned and looted property and attacked public institutions would be identified and prosecuted, and would have to pay for the damage. Macron ordered a review of intelligence gathering and public order planning before the protests. He said the government would prepare rules allowing prefects to ban certain gatherings in the coming days, and that it would bring students, teachers, lawmakers, trade unions and business leaders together this week to work on concrete proposals. He also said he would hold an emergency meeting with French mayors on Monday to discuss public safety. The Interior Ministry toll rose again on Sunday. In a message to mayors, Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu put arrests at 2,247, with 602 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters injured, up from the 1,800 arrests and more than 500 injured reported earlier in the day. The ministry counted 550,000 protesters nationwide, while organisers put the figure at 800,000. The Paris police prefecture said a man in his thirties suffered a cardiorespiratory arrest between Place de la Republique and Place de la Nation on Saturday and died in hospital.

    Updated 14:35 UTC, Oct 18, 2026. The cost of Saturday's unrest came into focus on Sunday. Alexandre Bompard, president of the Retail and Trade Federation, estimated that about 1,500 shops and retailers were directly hit, with losses put at several hundred million euros. President Emmanuel Macron is due to address the nation on television at 8 p.m. on Sunday. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu chairs an interministerial crisis meeting at 7 p.m. covering the economic impact, insurance and the judicial follow-up, and will also meet the mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon, who have demanded explanations over police resources. National Assembly President Yael Braun-Pivet is considering a parliamentary commission of inquiry and will meet parliamentary group leaders on Tuesday, BFMTV reported. Green leader Marine Tondelier, lawmaker Alexis Corbiere and the far-right National Rally have each called for Interior Minister Laurent Nunez to resign. Le Parisien reported that a 17-year-old lost a hand when struck by a police grenade in Saint-Etienne. Paris prosecutors reported 720 arrests in the capital, including 517 people held in custody, and said the figures were not yet final.

    Updated 10:32 UTC, Oct 18, 2026. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said on X on Sunday that 1,800 people had been arrested during and after the violent protests on Saturday, up from the 1,200 arrests Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu reported late on Saturday, and that investigations were under way to identify those responsible, AFP reported. More than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters were injured, Nunez said, up from 150 police officers hurt in the earlier count. One protester died after suffering a cardiac arrest during demonstrations in Paris, where police made a record 855 arrests, Reuters reported. A clean-up operation began on Sunday in cities from Lille to Marseille, Strasbourg and Bordeaux, where a masked group invaded the residence of the local prefect, Reuters reported. Bordeaux Mayor Thomas Cazenave said up to 100 stores had been looted in his city, and Marseille Mayor Benoit Payan announced emergency financial aid for businesses hit by the violence, Reuters reported.

    Updated 00:35 UTC, Oct 18, 2026. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said late on Saturday that 1,200 people had been arrested across France and 150 police officers had been hurt, The Times reported. The new arrest figure is three times the roughly 400 arrests Interior Minister Laurent Nunez had reported earlier in the evening, and The Sun also put the total at more than 1,200 arrested. Lecornu said far left groups had organised violence in several cities and that criminals had joined in to loot and destroy, and he defended officers who he said had faced extremely intense violence. In Saint-Etienne, the protester who lost a hand on Saturday was a 17 year old, The Times reported. An investigation is under way to establish whether the grenade that tore off his hand had been fired by the gendarmerie.

    Updated 21:09 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. The town hall in Limoges was largely lost to a fire set by masked protesters on Saturday, authorities told AFP. Prefect Maurice Barate said the roof of the 143 year old building had collapsed onto lower floors and that two-thirds of the exterior had already disappeared. He called the damage a catastrophe for heritage and blamed the fire on a deliberate act by hooligans, The Times reported. A witness told AFP the blaze had spread to one wing of the building and reached the roof, and firefighters were sent in to try to stop the flames consuming the whole town hall. Witnesses said a masked group had torched a police car outside the building before breaking windows, entering it and setting the fire. Several shops in the centre of Limoges were also looted, AFP reported.

    Updated 20:53 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. A protester in Saint-Etienne lost his hand on Saturday after a crowd control grenade exploded, a police source told AFP and BFMTV. It was the second such case in the current wave of protests, after a 15 year old lost his hand in Lens earlier this month. As night fell in Paris, groups of young people looted shops and started fires as the march ended, and police answered with tear gas and baton charges in narrow streets, CNN reported. President Emmanuel Macron wrote on X late on Saturday that there is no place for violence in the Republic and that people who broke, attacked and looted, including in town halls, will be brought to justice and punished. He said the nation stood with the officers who protect the right to protest.

    Updated 18:49 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said 550,000 people took part in protests across France on Saturday, double the number predicted, with 110,000 of them in Paris, and that about 400 people had been arrested, The Times reported. The CGT union put turnout at 500,000. In Paris, several hooded individuals got within about 150 yards of the Elysee Palace before riot police drove them back, and more than 300 people briefly put up construction barriers at the lower end of the Champs Elysees before police used tear gas and cleared the area. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said far left groups had organised violence in several cities and that criminals had joined in to loot and destroy, and he defended the police, saying officers had faced extremely intense violence. Marine Le Pen accused Jean Luc Melenchon's supporters of holding legitimate social demands hostage and seeking to plunge France into chaos. Melenchon blamed the authorities, saying they had orchestrated the violence by banning access to Place de la Republique and gassing it repeatedly.

    Updated 15:17 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Arrests across France rose to 272 by mid-afternoon, the interior ministry said, up from 166 at midday, after violent incidents in Paris, Marseille, Rennes, Toulouse and Angers. In Lyon, protesters set a tourist office on fire, and about 20,000 people joined the march in the city, AP reported. In Paris, a car and a newspaper kiosk were set on fire at Place de la Republique, where police fired tear gas and water cannon at masked protesters throwing fireworks. The interior ministry said the incidents included attempted looting, attacks on police, fires set to street furniture and attacks on seats of power and media outlets, and it blamed what it called the ultra-left.

    Updated 12:49 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Arrests across France rose to 166 by midday, the interior ministry said, with 100 of them in Paris alone, up from 61 detained in the Paris area in the morning. At least 32,800 people had already taken part in 140 demonstrations, and at least 170,000 people were expected on Saturday afternoon. In Marseille, a police station was vandalized and rioters entered city hall, Mayor Benoit Payan said. He told BFMTV the damage was fairly limited, but he called the act unacceptable. Tear gas hung over Place de la Republique in Paris, where two water cannon trucks stood ready and main roads into the square were blocked.

    Police detained 61 people in the Paris area on Saturday morning as a nationwide day of protests against the cost of living got under way across France, CNews reported, citing the police tally. AFP put the figure at around 60. Riot police fired tear gas in the capital before the main march started.

    The Yellow Vest movement called the day a "yellow and youth" protest and appealed to teachers, students, workers, pensioners and farmers to join on the first day of the half term holidays. Organisers said more than 600 demonstrations were planned. The main Paris march was set to start at 2 p.m. at Place de la Republique.

    Clashes in Marseille and Lyon

    In Marseille, protesters attacked a police building, an AFP photographer saw. Demonstrators clashed with police, who answered with tear gas, and briefly blocked a main road with rubbish bins and scooters before officers cleared it.

    In Lyon, the Rhone prefecture said police dispersed several groups of demonstrators, around forty people on the Guillotiere bridge, around fifteen at the Feyssine roundabout and around ten near the Feyzin refinery. Officers also moved in along the banks of the Rhone, where around a hundred people had gathered.

    In Rennes, around 20 people set up a barricade of trash bins and large pallets on the edge of a shopping mall. Christophe, a 47 year old carpenter and former Yellow Vest protester, told AFP he was angry after seeing what he called police brutality against teenagers in recent weeks.

    In Paris, authorities banned demonstrations near government buildings, the Elysee Palace, Matignon and the Champs Elysees, and around train stations. Police checked IDs outside Saint Lazare station. Protesters in balaclavas threw projectiles and broke windows at the headquarters of the television broadcaster CNews, AFP reported.

    From school protests to a wider movement

    Saturday follows weeks of high school blockades that began on Sep 18, 2026 in Creteil, a Paris suburb. Students have protested over teacher shortages, overcrowded classrooms and run down buildings. The protests have at times turned violent, and rights groups have accused police of using disproportionate force.

    The CGT union backs Saturday's protests, calling for higher wages and pensions, lower prices and public services that meet people's needs. A masked student in Marseille, who gave her name as Aude, said protesters were there over the poor resources given to education and against police brutality. A survey of 2,000 people published earlier this month found around 45 percent of French people worried about the cost of living and inflation, AFP reported.

    Macron appeals for calm

    President Emmanuel Macron appealed for calm on Friday, Oct 16, 2026 in Brussels. He said the right to protest had to go with respect for people and property, and he called people who come only to cause damage unacceptable.

    The day is a test of the mood in France a few months before the two round elections in April and May to choose Macron's successor. Far left leader Jean Luc Melenchon has backed the student protests and marched in a demonstration on Tuesday. Marine Le Pen, who leads opinion polls for the presidential race, has put her National Rally party on the side of the police, AFP reported.

    Interior Minister Laurent Nunez told France 2 that police could use stun grenades in case of extremely serious urban violence. Their use had been suspended during the student protests after a stun grenade blew off the hand of a 15 year old.
