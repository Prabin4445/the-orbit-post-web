    Canada Launches Judicial Symposium Tour to Widen Path to the Bench

    Ottawa and the Canadian Bar Association opened a national symposium series on October 22 to help strong lawyers see themselves as judges, with a first stop in Halifax on November 20.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 22, 2026 · 18:30 UTC2 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Canada's Minister of Justice and Attorney General, in partnership with the Canadian Bar Association, launched a national Judicial Symposium series in Ottawa on October 22, 2026.
    • The first symposium will take place in Halifax, Nova Scotia, on November 20, 2026.
    • Each symposium will bring members of the legal community together for practical talks on three subjects.

    Canada's Minister of Justice and Attorney General, in partnership with the Canadian Bar Association, launched a national Judicial Symposium series in Ottawa on October 22, 2026. The goal is direct. The government wants outstanding lawyers across the country to think seriously about judicial service and to consider putting their names forward for the bench.

    The first symposium will take place in Halifax, Nova Scotia, on November 20, 2026. More stops will follow across Canada in 2027. Lawyers who want to attend can register on the website of the Canadian Bar Association, known as the CBA.

    Advertisement

    What the sessions will cover

    Each symposium will bring members of the legal community together for practical talks on three subjects. The first is the role of an independent judiciary. The second is the federal judicial appointments process, step by step. The third is what serving as a judge means in daily practice.

    That mix matters. Many strong lawyers know courtrooms well as advocates. Fewer have a clear picture of how federal judges are chosen, what the work asks of a person, or how to apply. The series is built to close that gap with plain information, not ceremony.

    Why Ottawa is doing this

    An independent and highly qualified judiciary sits at the center of the rule of law. It also supports public confidence in the justice system. Courts ask people to accept decisions that go against them. That acceptance rests in part on trust that judges are qualified, independent, and drawn from the full strength of the legal profession.

    If talented lawyers never learn how the appointments process works, or never see themselves as candidates, the pool of applicants stays narrow. The department and the CBA say the series aims to encourage accomplished lawyers from a wide range of backgrounds, communities, and areas of practice to consider applying. A wider pool does not guarantee any single appointment. It does give the process more strong names to choose from.

    What happens next

    Halifax is the test run on November 20, 2026. Registrations run through the CBA website. Further stops across Canada will follow in 2027, which will take the same program to lawyers who cannot travel to Nova Scotia.

    The measure of the series will be simple. It will be whether lawyers who had not thought of the bench as a path for themselves start to do so, and whether future rounds of federal judicial appointments draw applicants from more places and more kinds of practice.
