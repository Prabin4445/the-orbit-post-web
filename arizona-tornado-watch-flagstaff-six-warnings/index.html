    Tornado Watch Covers Northern Arizona After Six Warnings in One Morning

    The National Weather Service put Coconino, Navajo and Yavapai counties under a Tornado Watch until 6 p.m. on Wednesday after six radar based tornado warnings in northern Arizona, while the same storms uprooted trees and damaged cars in Mesa on Tuesday night. No tornado had been confirmed on the ground.

    Key points

    • The National Weather Service in Flagstaff has finished its damage surveys and confirmed that two tornadoes touched down in northern Arizona on Wednesday, Oct 21.
    • The first tornado touched down near Prescott at 7:32 a.m. on Oct 21, with estimated peak winds of 105 mph.
    • The second tornado touched down in Heber-Overgaard at 6:59 p.m. the same day and was the stronger of the two, with estimated peak winds of 110 mph.

    Updated 01:18 UTC, Oct 25, 2026: Weather Service confirms two EF1 tornadoes in northern Arizona

    The National Weather Service in Flagstaff has finished its damage surveys and confirmed that two tornadoes touched down in northern Arizona on Wednesday, Oct 21. Both were rated EF1. The confirmation closes the open question in the updates below, which were written while survey teams were still working and no tornado had been confirmed on the ground.

    The first tornado touched down near Prescott at 7:32 a.m. on Oct 21, with estimated peak winds of 105 mph. It stayed on the ground for about 8 miles over 14 minutes and reached a width of about 300 yards, uprooting and snapping trees in the Bradshaw Mountains near Spruce Mountain and Walker Road. No injuries were reported from that storm.

    The second tornado touched down in Heber-Overgaard at 6:59 p.m. the same day and was the stronger of the two, with estimated peak winds of 110 mph. It traveled north across Highway 260 through the town and dissipated near Highway 277, covering 9.26 miles in 11 minutes at a width of about 350 yards. It damaged several homes and other buildings and uprooted and snapped trees. One person was injured: a man riding a bicycle was caught in the storm and taken to a hospital with injuries described as non-life-threatening.

    Fire officials in Heber-Overgaard received more than a dozen 911 calls in the first half hour, reporting downed power lines, damaged propane tanks and possible people trapped. Fire District Chief Jason Payne said fallen trees struck propane tanks and crews had to make the hazards safe, and most power in Overgaard was knocked out. Navajo County Sheriff David Clouse said residents near the path sheltered in closets and bathrooms. Cleanup is underway, and residents in the Heber-Overgaard area were still clearing damage on Oct 23.

    Updated 06:41 UTC, Oct 22, 2026: Possible tornado damages homes in Heber-Overgaard, emergency declaration requested

    A possible tornado struck the Heber-Overgaard area in Navajo County on Wednesday evening, damaging homes, knocking down power poles and cutting power, according to local officials and news reports. At least one person was injured. The National Weather Service has not confirmed that a tornado touched down.

    The National Weather Service in Flagstaff issued a tornado warning at 7:02 p.m. MST on October 21 for Navajo County. A severe thunderstorm capable of producing a tornado was over Heber-Overgaard, moving northeast at 40 mph, with rotation shown on radar and half dollar size hail. A severe thunderstorm warning for the same area ran until 7:30 p.m. MST.

    State Rep. Walt Blackman, who represents the area, called on Gov. Katie Hobbs to declare a state of emergency in Navajo County and send state resources. "The people of Heber need immediate assistance. We have families without power, significant property damage, and at least one reported injury," Blackman said. He said he plans to visit the area on Thursday afternoon, and listed his first tasks as restoring power, clearing blocked roads, helping displaced residents and securing state aid.

    The Navajo County Sheriff's Office said it was responding to a major incident in the Overgaard area and asked drivers to stay away from State Route 260 between Mogollon and the Overgaard Airpark, and from areas north of State Route 377 toward Tonto. Navopache Electric Cooperative reported outages, with no count of affected customers and no estimate for restoration. One Heber resident, Betty Nixon, told the Phoenix Focal that the wind and flying debris hit her home for about 30 to 45 seconds while she sheltered on the floor with her dogs. A large section of a neighbor's tree landed in her yard, and another neighbor's windows were reported shattered.

    Flooding hit the north of the state the same night. The National Weather Service in Flagstaff extended a Flash Flood Warning for the Moenkopi Wash in Coconino County until 2 a.m. MST on October 22. A river gauge read 33.54 feet at 8:45 p.m. MST on October 21, above major flood stage and well above the 27 foot flood stage. Forecasters said life threatening flash flooding was already happening around Moenkopi and Tuba City, where the water can reach homes in lower Moenkopi Village, and more heavy rain was expected overnight.

    Updated 02:45 UTC, Oct 22, 2026: Watch expires, no tornado confirmed

    The Tornado Watch for Coconino, Navajo and Yavapai counties expired at 6 p.m. MST on Wednesday, and no tornado was confirmed on the ground anywhere in northern Arizona during the day. Every warning on Wednesday was based on rotation seen on radar.

    The storms still landed hard in Flagstaff. Hail the size of golf balls, 1.75 inches across, was reported in the downtown area, and a gust of 72 mph was recorded at Flagstaff Airport, The Weather Channel reported in its recap of the day. The watch was the first tornado watch issued for northern Arizona in more than 16 years.

    The wider system is not done. Forecasters said a flood risk runs through Thursday across the Southwest, and Arizona again sits under the greatest severe storm risk in the region on Thursday.

    The National Weather Service in Flagstaff placed Coconino, Navajo and Yavapai counties under a Tornado Watch on Wednesday. The watch took effect at 10:10 a.m. MST and runs until 6:00 p.m. MST on October 21. It followed six tornado warnings in northern Arizona that morning. No tornado had been confirmed on the ground. The same storm system had hit Mesa the night before, where wind uprooted trees and damaged cars on Tuesday night.

    The first warnings came early. Forecasters issued the first three tornado warnings around 5 a.m. for parts of Coconino County and Yavapai County. Another warning followed for the Prescott area shortly before 8 a.m. A warning for Jerome came soon after. Each warning pointed to rotation seen on radar, not a tornado seen on the ground.

    Flagstaff warning covers downtown

    The sixth warning came at 9:22 a.m. MST. It covered downtown Flagstaff. The storm was over the city and moving north at 45 mph, with quarter size hail. The warning area included Northern Arizona University, East Flagstaff, Fort Valley and Doney Park. It also covered stretches of Interstate 40 and Interstate 17. The warning expired at 9:45 a.m.

    An earlier warning at 5:49 a.m. had covered an area near Fort Valley, about 11 miles northwest of Flagstaff. That storm was moving northeast at 35 mph. All six warnings on Wednesday morning were radar based. Forecasters said they saw rotation in the storms. By late morning, no tornado had been confirmed on the ground in northern Arizona.

    Mesa damage from Tuesday night storms

    In Mesa, residents at the Talise Apartments near Gilbert Road and University Drive woke to damage from Tuesday night. Wind uprooted trees at the complex. A branch fell on a carport, bent the structure and damaged a vehicle below. Other trees fell on cars, and windshields shattered.

    The National Weather Service in Phoenix had issued a severe thunderstorm warning for Maricopa County that ran until 10:30 p.m. on Tuesday. The storm was over the Fiesta Mall area, about five miles east of Tempe, and moving northeast at 35 mph. Forecasters listed gusts of 60 mph and hail up to 1.25 inches.

    Hail, wind and flooding lead the rest of Wednesday

    Forecasters said the biggest risk for the rest of Wednesday was large hail over an inch across. Hail of two inches or more was possible in southern Coconino County, including Flagstaff, in eastern Yavapai County, including Prescott, and in northern Gila County, including Payson. Damaging winds over 60 mph were also possible.

    The tornado chance was put at 5 percent, in an area from western Mohave County to northern Gila County and northeastern Maricopa County. Rain could fall at one to two inches an hour. That rate raises the risk of flash flooding. A Flood Watch runs through Thursday evening for northern and central Arizona.

    Watch means be ready, warning means act

    A watch means be ready. A warning means act. If a warning is issued for your area, go to an interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building and stay away from windows. Do not wait to see the storm. Never drive across a flooded road. Turn around and find another route.
