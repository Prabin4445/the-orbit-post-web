    El Paso, Texas: Lightning and Hail Halt Friday Night Football, Two Games Moved to Saturday

    Lightning and hail delayed high school football games across five El Paso area school districts on Friday night, October 23, 2026, with Parkland against Hanks moved to 9 a.m. Saturday and Del Valle against Burges to 10 a.m., under a severe thunderstorm watch until 9 p.m.

    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Lightning and hail stopped Friday night football across El Paso on October 23, 2026, as severe storms moved over the Borderland at kickoff time.
    • Two games were moved outright to Saturday, October 24.
    • A severe thunderstorm watch was in effect for El Paso and much of New Mexico until 9 p.m. on Friday, October 23.

    Lightning and hail stopped Friday night football across El Paso on October 23, 2026, as severe storms moved over the Borderland at kickoff time. The El Paso Times reported that games at campuses in five school districts were delayed: the El Paso, Ysleta, San Elizario, Clint and Socorro independent school districts. El Paso games typically kick off at 7 p.m., and by the time the storms settled over the city, the normal Friday night schedule was no longer possible.

    Two games move to Saturday morning

    Two games were moved outright to Saturday, October 24. Parkland and Hanks, both Ysleta district schools, rescheduled their game for 9 a.m. Saturday. Del Valle and Burges moved their game to 10 a.m. Saturday. The El Paso Times reported that if the remaining games could not be played on Friday night, they would be played on Saturday, October 24, which leaves coaches, players and families waiting on the Saturday forecast as well as the Friday storms.

    The storm behind the delays

    A severe thunderstorm watch was in effect for El Paso and much of New Mexico until 9 p.m. on Friday, October 23. The El Paso Times reported that the storms could produce wind gusts of up to 70 mph, large hail and frequent lightning, the exact combination that makes an outdoor stadium unsafe and a field unplayable. Heavy rain was also expected, with a chance of flash flooding. The National Weather Service said the flash flood threat was a particular concern because soils across the region remain saturated from heavy rainfall in late September.

    The watch also covered several New Mexico communities, including Albuquerque, Santa Fe, Las Cruces, Alamogordo, Ruidoso, Roswell, Silver City and Farmington. Weather.com's El Paso page listed flash flood warnings issued on Friday evening, at 5:56 p.m., 6:04 p.m. and 6:32 p.m. Mountain time, running until 9 p.m. and 9:30 p.m. Mountain time. That warning window covers the hours when the football games would have been played.

    What a watch means for the rest of the night

    A severe thunderstorm watch means conditions are favorable for severe thunderstorms to develop. The El Paso Times, citing the weather service guidance, said residents should monitor weather updates and be prepared to seek shelter if a warning is issued. The paper's flood safety guidance told readers to avoid floodwaters, to get to higher ground if they live in a flood prone area, and to obey evacuation orders if officials issue them. Six inches of moving water can knock a person off their feet, the guidance noted, and water levels and flow rates can change quickly during a flood.

    For the football programs, the cost of the night is a short week turned shorter. Teams that move to Saturday morning lose their normal Friday night routine and part of their weekend recovery, and they play in daylight heat instead of under lights. For families, the change is simpler and closer to home: the biggest social night of the school week in El Paso was called off by the sky, and the makeup plan starts early on Saturday.
