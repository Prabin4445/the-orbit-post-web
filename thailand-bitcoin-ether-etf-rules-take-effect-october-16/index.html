    Thailand Bitcoin and Ether ETF Rules Take Effect October 16

    Thailand will allow locally listed Bitcoin and Ether funds from October 16 under 11 new rules. CoinShares expects a slow start, with no fund approved yet.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 15, 2026 · 18:25 UTC2 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Thailand opens a new door for crypto investors on Friday.
    • Only two assets qualify in the first phase.
    • Investors must acknowledge the risks of crypto funds before they trade.

    Thailand opens a new door for crypto investors on Friday. Rules for locally listed Bitcoin and Ether exchange traded funds take effect on October 16, the Securities and Exchange Commission said in a statement first issued on October 8. The framework covers 11 notifications and sets the terms for the first crypto funds to trade on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.

    Only two assets qualify in the first phase. Each fund must track one asset, Bitcoin or Ether, and hold average net exposure of at least 80 percent of its net asset value to that asset across each accounting year. The coins must sit with custodians licensed by the Thai SEC. Funds trade only on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.

    Advertisement

    What the rules require

    Investors must acknowledge the risks of crypto funds before they trade. Brokers may not lend clients money to buy the funds. Asset managers can outsource work only to licensed digital asset fund managers, and each product still needs its own approval before trading begins. October 16 is the start date for the rulebook, not a guaranteed launch day for any fund.

    Thai mutual funds and private funds can buy the local products under existing investment limits. Until now, they could reach Bitcoin only through foreign funds. The new rules narrow that foreign route at the same time. Depositary receipts tied to foreign crypto ETFs are banned in the first phase, and brokers can arrange foreign crypto ETF purchases only for institutional and ultra high net worth clients.

    Why it matters

    The design pulls Thai crypto demand onshore, into funds that Thai regulators supervise and Thai custodians hold. It also arrives in a soft market. CoinShares told CoinTelegraph it expects modest inflows at first, because existing investment options already compete for the same money and issuers still face regulatory hurdles. Thai trading activity has cooled this year, a point CoinCentral noted in its report on the final rules.

    Bitcoin has had a hard week on this site's markets coverage, falling below 81,000 dollars as liquidations neared 1 billion dollars. A regulated local fund does not remove that price risk. It changes who holds the coins, what disclosures investors get, and which regulator watches the product.

    What happens next

    Watch for the first asset manager to win approval and name a launch date. The SEC said it may consider more digital assets later, based on liquidity, market acceptance, blockchain security and investor protection. For now, the list stops at two, and the first funds have yet to appear.

    Timeline

    1. Oct 8, 2026Thailand's SEC issued 11 notifications setting the framework for local Bitcoin and Ether ETFs.
    2. Oct 15, 2026CoinShares said it expects modest initial inflows, with no fund approved yet.
    3. Oct 16, 2026The ETF rules take effect. Funds trade only on the Stock Exchange of Thailand once approved.
