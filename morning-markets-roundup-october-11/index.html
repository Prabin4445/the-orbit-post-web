    Saudi Stocks Dip After Attack as Oil Holds Above $100

    Saudi stocks fell a modest 1% after the Riyadh airport attack while oil held above $100; India foreign outflows topped Rs 3 lakh crore; crypto stayed flat. Here is what it means for ordinary money.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 11, 2026 · 14:30 UTC3 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Saudi Arabia's stock market fell on Sunday after the attack on Riyadh airport, but the selloff was modest for a market worth around $2 trillion.
    • Crypto did nothing overnight.
    • A 1% drop in TASI and 0.6% in Aramco is the market's way of pricing a one-off event, not a war.

    Saudi Arabia's stock market fell on Sunday after the attack on Riyadh airport, but the selloff was modest for a market worth around $2 trillion. The benchmark TASI index dropped 1%. Aramco slipped 0.6%. Saudi National Bank lost 1%. Qatar's index fell 1% as well. Organizers said two major Saudi energy and investment conferences scheduled for this month will still go ahead.

    Crypto did nothing overnight. Bitcoin traded near $83,000, Ethereum around $2,500, Solana near $109, and BNB near $748, according to overnight wire snapshots through 09:15 CDT. All four were essentially flat against 24 hours earlier. That is the whole crypto story this morning, and there is no reason to dress it up further.

    Advertisement

    Why a missile attack barely dented a $2 trillion market

    A 1% drop in TASI and 0.6% in Aramco is the market's way of pricing a one-off event, not a war. Aramco's production was not reported damaged, oil kept flowing, and the conferences going ahead as planned gave investors a reason to treat Sunday as a shock rather than a repricing. Compare it to a real supply disruption and the numbers make sense: markets punish uncertainty about cash flows, and Sunday offered very little of it.

    The steadier tell is oil itself. Brent sits above $100, kept high by Gulf supply risk after the Riyadh attack. That is the number that reaches ordinary budgets. Oil above $100 means higher gasoline prices at the pump, higher diesel costs in every supply chain, and upward pressure on inflation, which is the number that keeps mortgage rates high. A quiet crypto market and an expensive oil market rarely feel the same in a household budget, but the expensive oil is the one doing the damage.

    India's slow-motion exit

    Foreign portfolio investors pulled Rs 44,166 crore from Indian equities in October so far, PTI reported via The Hindu BusinessLine. Total 2026 outflows have now crossed Rs 3.04 lakh crore, against Rs 1.66 lakh crore in all of 2025.

    The drivers are a textbook rotation. Elevated crude raises India's import bill. A firm dollar and high U.S. yields make American assets pay more with less risk. And the AI-led rally in North Asia is pulling capital toward that region instead. When the U.S. pays 5% on safe money, emerging-market equities need a strong story to keep foreign money parked, and India's story is being drowned out by oil prices and regional competition.

    For anyone holding India exposure in a retirement portfolio, the FPI numbers are the signal to read, not the daily index wiggles. Sustained foreign selling of this size is not noise. It is a vote on relative returns, and it usually runs for months, not weeks.

    Key numbers

    • 1%: TASI index drop on Sunday; Aramco down 0.6%, Saudi National Bank down 1%, Qatar's index down 1%
    • Rs 44,166 crore: FPI outflow from Indian equities in October so far
    • Rs 3.04 lakh crore: total 2026 FPI outflow from Indian equities, vs Rs 1.66 lakh crore in all of 2025
    • $83,000 / $2,500 / $109 / $748: BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, all essentially flat over 24 hours
    • $100+: Brent crude, held up by Gulf supply risk

    What happens next

    This week's calendar carries the catalysts that could move these stories. India gets Q2 earnings from Wipro and HCL Tech plus September CPI inflation data. The U.S. releases inflation and retail sales figures. Oil traders will watch whether Gulf tensions stay contained or widen, since Brent above $100 with a supply scare underneath it is a market waiting for a second shock.

    The honest read: equity markets in the Gulf shrugged, Indian equities are in a genuine foreign-money drought, and crypto is asleep. A quiet tape is still information. It tells you where the risk is not, which is half the job.
