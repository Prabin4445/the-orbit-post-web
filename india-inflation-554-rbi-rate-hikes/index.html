    India Inflation Hits 5.54 Percent as Stocks Rally Anyway

    Consumer prices rose 5.54 percent in September, with food inflation at 7.74 percent, even as Mumbai stocks surged on technology earnings. The RBI has already raised rates once and economists expect more.

    NEW DELHI: India got two economic stories on the same morning, and they pulled against each other. Stock markets in Mumbai surged on strong technology earnings, with the Sensex closing up more than 1,100 points on Tuesday. The inflation data told a harder story. Consumer prices rose 5.54 percent in September from a year earlier, the fastest pace this year, close to the top of the central bank tolerance band and high enough to keep talk of more rate hikes alive.

    Readers searching for either story need both halves. The rally was real. So is the squeeze on household budgets that sits behind it.

    Advertisement

    The inflation number, in plain terms

    Retail inflation accelerated to 5.54 percent in September, up from 4.83 percent in August, according to data released on Monday by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation. A Reuters poll of economists had expected 5.4 percent. September was the fourth straight month above the Reserve Bank of India medium term target of 4 percent, and the reading sits close to the 6 percent ceiling of the tolerance band that runs from 2 to 6 percent.

    Food did most of the damage. Food inflation jumped to 7.74 percent in September from 5.97 percent in August. Prices for staples such as onion, ginger and garlic rose sharply. Base effects added to the print, because prices a year earlier were low. Inflation in rural areas reached 6.08 percent, above the ceiling, while urban inflation was 4.88 percent. Personal care, restaurants and accommodation also rose fast.

    One analysis of the release put the September figure at a 23 month high. However it is measured, the direction is clear. Prices are rising faster than the central bank wants, and food is leading.

    The central bank already moved

    The RBI raised its benchmark repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5 percent last week, its first increase in nearly four years. The last hike before that came in February 2023. Governor Sanjay Malhotra said headline inflation is expected to average nearly 5.8 percent over the next three quarters. The bank raised its inflation forecast for fiscal 2027 to 5.2 percent, from 5 percent earlier.

    Malhotra also warned that a weak monsoon and strong El Nino conditions pose risks to crops and rural demand, while noting that ample foodgrain stocks and government steps should cushion the blow. Alexandra Hermann Prasad, lead economist at Oxford Economics, said the data reinforced the case for more hikes and forecast two further increases of 25 basis points each, in December and February. Other analysts quoted on Tuesday warned that if food and energy prices stay high, borrowing costs will stay higher for longer.

    Why markets rallied anyway

    Mumbai stocks had their own reasons to climb. HCLTech reported quarterly profit up about 21 percent and TCS also posted strong results, and technology shares led the market higher. The Sensex closed at 73,037.72, up 1.55 percent, and the Nifty 50 rose 1.52 percent to 22,702.40. Traders were pricing company earnings, not the cost of onions.

    There were limits to the cheer. Foreign investors sold Indian shares for a 12th straight session on Monday, offloading about 17.08 billion rupees on the day, and October outflows reached about 4.9 billion dollars in provisional figures reported by Reuters. Brent crude held near 105 dollars a barrel on Middle East supply risk, which feeds straight into Indian fuel and food costs. The RBI also tightened rules on rupee derivative contracts in recent days as it works to support a currency weakened by oil prices and outflows.

    Key numbers

    • September retail inflation: 5.54 percent, up from 4.83 percent in August
    • Economists forecast for September: 5.4 percent
    • Food inflation: 7.74 percent, up from 5.97 percent
    • Rural inflation: 6.08 percent. Urban inflation: 4.88 percent
    • RBI target: 4 percent, with a tolerance band of 2 to 6 percent
    • Repo rate: 5.5 percent after a 25 basis point hike last week
    • RBI fiscal 2027 inflation forecast: 5.2 percent, raised from 5 percent
    • Sensex close on Tuesday: 73,037.72, up 1.55 percent
    • Foreign selling in October: about 4.9 billion dollars so far

    What it means for households

    For a family in India, this story lands in the kitchen first. Onion, ginger and garlic are not luxuries. When food inflation runs near 8 percent, weekly budgets bend fast, and rural households feel it hardest, with rural inflation already above 6 percent. The second hit comes through loans. If the RBI hikes again in December, home loan and vehicle loan rates drift up or stay high, while deposit rates also stay better for savers. The rally helps people with market linked savings and pension funds. It does not pay the vegetable bill.

    What happens next

    The next tests come in order. October inflation data will show whether September was a spike or a trend. The RBI meets again in December, with Oxford Economics expecting a hike and markets watching food prices, the monsoon outcome and oil. If crude stays near 105 dollars and food stays hot, the case for tighter policy gets stronger. If harvest supplies ease food prices, the bank gets room to wait. Both the rally and the rate debate now run on the same two inputs: food and fuel.
