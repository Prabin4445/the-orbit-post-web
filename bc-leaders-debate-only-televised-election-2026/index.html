    BC Leaders Face Off in Only Televised Debate of Snap Election

    David Eby, Lorne Doerkson and Emily Lowan debated in Vancouver on Wednesday night, ten days before British Columbians vote on October 24. The deficit, taxes and the cost of living framed the only head to head meeting of the campaign.

    British Columbia's three main party leaders met Wednesday night in Vancouver for the only televised debate of the snap provincial election, ten days before voters go to the polls on October 24.

    NDP Leader David Eby, interim Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson and Green Leader Emily Lowan debated from 6:15 p.m. to 8 p.m. Pacific, carried on all major television and radio networks. Shachi Kurl, president of the Angus Reid Institute, moderated. With no second debate scheduled, the evening was the one chance for undecided voters to see the three leaders answer the same questions on the same stage.

    The money fight came first

    The campaign has turned on the province's books. The NDP released its platform costings hours before the debate. They show a deficit of about $12 billion by 2028-2029, down from a forecast of almost $14 billion this fiscal year. The plan leans on about $1 billion in new revenue from income tax increases on the top 3.88 percent of earners, plus $730 million from targeted economic growth, and claims a platform surplus of $581 million by 2028-2029 once the measures are combined with earlier forecasts.

    The Greens released their platform on Tuesday. It promises new spending on health care, education and social services worth almost $9.3 billion a year over the next two fiscal years, paid for in part by a one time tax on excessive wealth that the party says would raise $22.2 billion in its first year. The Conservatives had not released costed figures by debate day, a gap both rivals pressed.

    What business told the leaders on Tuesday

    The debate followed a sharper exchange in front of the Greater Vancouver Board of Trade on Tuesday, where the same three leaders pitched competing economic plans. Doerkson promised tax cuts, a 120 day approval process for capital projects worth more than $250 million, and a three year provincial sales tax break on machinery, equipment and software. Eby pledged a BC shipyard to build future ferries at home and said his party would regulate profits on basic groceries and fuel. Lowan argued for an industrial strategy that leans less on natural resource extraction, funded by the Greens' wealth tax.

    The board's own survey set the tone: 78 percent of responding members said the provincial economy is heading in the wrong direction, and 74 percent raised concerns about the deficit, forecast at $13.8 billion.

    Why it matters

    BC elections usually turn on housing and health care. This one is running through the deficit and the trade fight with the United States, which has squeezed the province's forest sector and exporters. Eby is asking voters to keep a steady hand in a trade war. Doerkson, an interim leader most voters barely knew before the writ dropped, is asking them to change course. Lowan, at 26 the youngest leader on the stage, is asking them to tax wealth and spend the proceeds on services.

    What happens next

    Advance voting runs in the final stretch before election day on Saturday, October 24. The next test for all three campaigns is whether Wednesday night moved anyone: post debate polling in the coming days will show if the single debate changed a race that the costings fight has so far defined.
