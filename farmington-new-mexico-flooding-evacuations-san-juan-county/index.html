    Farmington, New Mexico: Flooding Forces Evacuations Across San Juan County as Rivers Rise Well Above Flood Stage

    The San Juan River at Farmington reached 13.2 feet on Friday morning against a flood stage of 8.5 feet. About 600 households were told to evacuate, an estimated 1,500 homes and buildings were affected, schools closed, and a wastewater washout at Bloomfield sent raw sewage into the river.

    Flooding on the San Juan and Animas rivers forced evacuations across San Juan County on Friday, October 23, and closed schools in Farmington, Bloomfield and Aztec. Officials said no deaths had been reported by Friday afternoon, and Farmington Police Chief Steve Hebbe called it a once in a generation event at a Friday press conference.

    Updated 20:57 UTC, October 24

    The flood crest moved downstream to Shiprock on Saturday. The Shiprock bridge over the San Juan River was shut down in the early morning hours of October 24, according to the Navajo Nation Police Department, and rescue operations were carried out overnight to bring people in flooded areas to safety. First responders carried out 20 rescues overnight in the Farmington and Shiprock area, with multiple people involved in several of those operations, and San Juan County emergency management said the department staged 20 rescues while about 1,500 people were without power, according to local utilities.

    Advertisement

    A two mile stretch of U.S. Highway 64 west of Shiprock into the town was closed because of standing water on the roadway, the New Mexico Department of Transportation said, and standing water and flooding over a bridge over the San Juan River closed a four mile stretch of New Mexico 371 south of Farmington. Navajo Nation President Buu Nygren said residents had been stranded on rooftops and others needed evacuation assistance in the Shiprock area, KOAT reported, and a shelter threatened by rising water near Shiprock was closed and its occupants were moved to another location. Power outages were reported south of the San Juan River in Shiprock, and the Four Corners Monument remained closed on Saturday after the Animas River crested and flooded the area on Friday.

    "We may see some floodwaters receding, but there will be more rain, and the rivers are expected to rise again," San Juan County Emergency Management warned in a Facebook post on Saturday. Farmington police asked residents on Saturday to stay home and off the roads as crews responded to flood damage, saying many roadways had sustained damage and continued traffic could make it worse. No new death toll was reported in the Saturday updates.

    Heavy rain fell on mountain snow on Thursday and the runoff pushed both rivers up fast. The San Juan River at Farmington reached 13.2 feet at 10 a.m. Mountain time on Friday, well above its 8.5 foot flood stage, and forecasters expected a crest near 14 feet on Friday afternoon, with the river staying above flood stage into Saturday.

    Evacuations and a shelter at McGee Park

    About 600 households in Farmington were told to evacuate, city spokesperson Gina Intoppa told The Associated Press. San Juan County listed evacuated areas including Wildhorse Valley, Lee Acres, parts of Bloomfield near South Church and Calle Del Rio, County Road 2620 and other roads along the Animas, and in Farmington the Rancho de Animas area, Pryor Lane and homes along either river. Evacuees were sent to McGee Park, where pets and recreational vehicles are welcome. Farmington, Bloomfield and Aztec schools and San Juan College all closed on Friday. Road closures were, in the words of police quoted by the Tri-City Record, too many to mention.

    Rescues by boat, helicopter and amphibious vehicle

    The National Weather Service estimated that as many as 1,500 homes and buildings had been affected and that about 60 water rescues had taken place since Thursday afternoon. County spokesperson Devin Neeley said New Mexico Guard crews were using boats, helicopters and amphibious vehicles to reach people in homes holding several feet of water and people on rooftops. "This is a once in a generation time. This is singular and this is real," Hebbe said. "So far, we have not had any deaths, but this is not anything that should be played with."

    A wastewater washout puts sewage in the river

    The flooding washed out part of the Bloomfield wastewater treatment plant into the San Juan River, sending raw sewage into the floodwater. Neeley called the river a hazmat situation and told residents not to touch the water. Bloomfield asked residents and businesses to use as little water as possible, postponing laundry and dishwashers and keeping showers brief. Bloomfield Mayor John Mohler said it was the highest water he had seen in a long time. Farmington's wastewater plant was being watched and still had a buffer of about 6 feet. Valves to Farmington Lake were closed on Thursday night, and Intoppa said the lake was 89.14 percent full and not in danger of flooding.

    What officials expect next

    Rain on Friday was expected to be much lighter, but runoff was still converging into the rivers and levels were still rising in places. The county emergency management office warned that more areas could flood and told residents not to wait if they saw rising water. Officials urged everyone to stay home unless travel was necessary and to never drive through flooded roads.
