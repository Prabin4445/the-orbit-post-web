    Wainwright Fire Started on Grenade Launcher Range, Military Says

    Officials said on Thursday that the Oct. 17 fire at CFB Wainwright began on the automatic grenade launcher range during routine training, stayed in a danger area firefighters could not enter, then raced into another training area when the wind shifted. Twenty seven of the 39 injured remain in hospital.

    Updated 05:28 UTC, Oct. 23, 2026

    Canadian military officials said on Thursday, Oct. 22, 2026 that the fire that killed four soldiers at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright started on the base automatic grenade launcher range during routine training, Reuters reported. Brig.-Gen. Vanessa Hanrahan, the Canadian Forces Provost Marshal, told reporters the fire initiated on that range as a result of the routine military training that was happening there at the time. She did not say whether live firing of explosive rounds ignited it, The Globe and Mail reported.

    Hanrahan said the fire first stayed inside Permanent Danger Area 2 of the range, an area that can be contaminated with unexploded munitions. Firefighters could not attack it directly, so range control and the base firehall monitored it through the day. "A subsequent change in wind direction and velocity significantly increased the fire's intensity," she said. The fire then spread to a different training area and overran the positions of soldiers practising attacks on simulated enemy positions.

    Advertisement

    The Canadian Armed Forces said 27 of the 39 injured remained in hospital on Thursday and 12 had been treated and released. Canadian Press reported 13 of those still in hospital were in critical condition. About 84 soldiers were split between the grenade range and the second training area that day, drawn from Charlie Company of the 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry, and they were training ahead of a planned deployment to Latvia.

    Hanrahan said about 30 military police are examining witness accounts and the scene, and that there is no evidence of a criminal cause so far and no indication that natural gas infrastructure was involved. Three investigations are now under way, run by the military police, a military board of inquiry and Alberta's Chief Medical Examiner, and officials said the work is expected to take longer than the roughly one month first suggested. Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland told reporters in Edmonton on Thursday that the military is committed to an honest account of what led to the fire. Environment Canada said a fast moving cold front on Saturday, Oct. 17 brought winds between 90 and 130 kilometres per hour over much of Alberta, and a yellow wind warning was issued for Wainwright at 2:14 p.m. local time that day.

    Sources

    Reuters, The Globe and Mail via NewsBeep, CBC News, Canadian Press via EuropeSays

    Updated 21:28 UTC, Oct. 19, 2026

    Prime Minister Mark Carney told Parliament on Monday, Oct. 19, 2026 that the cause of the fire is still not known and the source of ignition has not been determined, Reuters reported. Carney said 50 investigators are on the ground, from the army and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police, to determine how the disaster happened. CNN reported that the full investigation is led by the Canadian Forces National Investigation Service, supported by the RCMP.

    Carney said 39 people remain in hospital, with 19 in critical condition, Reuters reported. "This loss touches every corner of the nation," he said. Members of Parliament observed a moment of silence, and party leaders paid tribute in the House of Commons, Global News reported. King Charles and Queen Camilla said they were "greatly shocked and saddened" and sent their deepest sympathy to the families of those who lost their lives, and to all those who have been injured, Sky News reported.

    Updated 04:54 UTC, Oct. 19, 2026

    Reuters reported on Oct. 19, 2026 that 85 soldiers from the 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry, were in high intensity combat training with live munitions when the fire began on Saturday. Winds were below 50 kilometres per hour leading up to the fire, then rose to as high as 96 kilometres per hour, Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland told reporters on Sunday.

    Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said many of the injured will require weeks and months of specialized, intensive medical care, Reuters reported. The BBC reported that the fire is now contained and that fire and rescue teams are staying at the scene to watch for flare ups. Rutland expected a full investigation into the fire to take at least a month, CBC News reported.

    Updated 22:57 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland named the four service members killed in the CFB Wainwright fire at a press conference on Sunday afternoon. They are Pte. Aidan Goulding, from North Vancouver, Pte. Shaun Farrell, from Prince Albert, Saskatchewan, Pte. Hayden Lykkemark, from Langley, British Columbia, and Sgt. Morgan Wong, from Edmonton, CBC News reported. CNN reported that Rutland identified the dead as three privates and a sergeant.

    Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said 39 people were in hospital on Sunday afternoon and at least 19 were in critical condition, CNN reported. A provincial health official said eight of the injured were stable and 12 had lesser injuries and could walk. The code orange at Alberta hospitals had been lifted by Sunday afternoon, CNN reported. Emergency Health Services Alberta sent 30 units to Wainwright, including 22 ground ambulances, six fixed-wing air ambulances and two helicopters.

    Rutland said a grass fire ignited during a training activity involving Charlie Company of the 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry. The troops were taking part in section attack and grenade exercises, part of high-intensity combat training with live munitions. A fire had been burning for several hours about one kilometre away, he said, and range control let the training continue because that fire was contained and was not expected to move near the soldiers. Then uncommonly strong winds shifted the fire, and it covered the kilometre in 30 to 60 seconds. "It is not normal for a fire to move a kilometer in 30, 60 seconds," Rutland said, adding that he had never seen anything like it.

    Rutland said there were no explosions, the cause is still unknown, and investigators cannot yet say whether munitions played a part. He said the military does not believe there was any outside interference and does not believe oil and gas infrastructure was involved. That rules out the gas line account that unnamed military sources gave to Global News earlier on Sunday, which officials had never confirmed. An investigation will decide whether the tragedy was preventable, Rutland said. The battalion was preparing to become a rapid reaction force and to deploy to Latvia in the spring as part of Canada's NATO mission, CNN reported.

    Updated 19:30 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    The Canadian Armed Forces said on Sunday that there is no indication the fire at CFB Wainwright was the result of terrorist activity, and that the incident remains under investigation, the Canadian Press reported. The Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire was being held late on Saturday and described it as under control on Sunday. CBC News reported that crews remained at work in the training area and residents were asked to stay away. Defence Minister David McGuinty visited the Wainwright Health Centre on Sunday and met families of victims and town leaders, CBC reported. The Department of National Defence said the names of the four dead service members will not be released until all next of kin have been notified. Prime Minister Mark Carney said on Sunday that officials on the ground would give a detailed account and a formal briefing later on Sunday. That detailed briefing had not yet taken place by Sunday afternoon in Wainwright, and officials have still not released an official count of the injured. Alberta Health Services has said 20 people were admitted as inpatients on Saturday. Higher injury counts in some reports rest on unnamed military sources and have not been confirmed by officials.

    Updated 17:07 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed in a public address on Sunday afternoon that four service members were killed and dozens more were seriously injured in the fire at CFB Wainwright, PA Media reported. Carney said the families of those killed had been notified, that the situation was still evolving, and that senior military and government officials would give a detailed account of the incident later on Sunday.

    Carney said Canadian flags would fly at half mast on federal buildings across the country. "All Canadians stand with the service members at CFB Wainwright, their families, and their community," he said. The Sun reported that Carney described the fire as one of the worst peacetime tragedies for Canada's military on domestic soil, and said the nation is in mourning.

    The prime minister's confirmed toll of four is the first official death count from the incident. It replaces the position in earlier updates, where officials had released no official number. The at least seven deaths reported by Global News, citing military sources, have not been confirmed by officials, and no cause has been officially confirmed.

    Updated 14:52 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    Wainwright Mayor Bruce Pugh described the incident as the largest mass casualty event the community has ever seen, The Sun reported on Oct. 18, 2026.

    More than a dozen Canadian Armed Forces personnel parachuted into Wainwright on Saturday night, Oct. 17, 2026, arriving at the local health centre to help respond to the explosion and fire, Global News senior correspondent Jeff Semple reported on X. Canadian Press photographs also showed search and rescue technicians deployed by parachute to the area to provide additional care.

    The Sun reported winds reached 62 miles per hour as flames were driven beyond the base. Officials still have not released an official death or injury count, and they still have not confirmed a cause. The at least seven deaths reported by Global News, citing military sources, remain unconfirmed by officials.

    Updated 09:16 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    The Canadian Armed Forces confirmed in a news release dated Oct. 18, 2026 that the incident at CFB Wainwright resulted in fatalities and injuries. The release said a fire ignited within the training area of the base at about 4:00 p.m. local time on Oct. 17, 2026 and spread rapidly beyond the boundaries of the training area, while military and civilian emergency services responded.

    The Armed Forces said the full extent of the impact is still under assessment. Supporting those affected and notifying next of kin is the immediate priority, it said, and it will not confirm casualty or injury details until that process is complete. Additional information will be provided as it becomes available, the release said. The official toll has still not been released, so the at least seven deaths reported by Global News, citing military sources, remain unconfirmed by officials, and no cause has been officially confirmed.

    Updated 07:27 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    At least seven people are dead and dozens are injured after the explosion and fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, multiple military sources told Global News, in a report published on Oct. 18, 2026. Officials have not confirmed that toll, and Defence Minister David McGuinty has still not given an official number for the dead or injured.

    The same sources told Global News that a live fire training exercise hit a gas line or a gas well on the base and set off an explosion. Officials have not confirmed a cause, and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police investigation is still under way.

    Late on Saturday, Oct. 17, 2026, the Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire was being held, and The Sun reported that by 11 p.m. local time authorities said property and critical infrastructure were protected. No evacuation order was issued for the town.

    Edmonton hospitals declared a code orange, the disaster protocol used for a high number of patients, CBC reported. Dr. Warren Thirsk, an emergency doctor at the Royal Alexandra Hospital, told CBC that Wainwright health facilities were completely overwhelmed and called it a disaster that comes once in 15 to 25 years. Patients were moved to hospitals across Edmonton. Saskatchewan sent resources to help, Premier Scott Moe said, and the Shock Trauma Air Rescue Service sent helicopters from Edmonton and Saskatoon.

    Updated 03:26 UTC, Oct. 18, 2026

    Defence Minister David McGuinty said late on Saturday that people were killed and injured in the incident at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright. "I'm saddened to share that there have been both fatalities and injuries as a result of an incident on the base," McGuinty said, the Associated Press and CNN reported.

    McGuinty gave no numbers for the dead or injured and did not say what caused the incident. He described the situation as "very, very active and delicate," said the Royal Canadian Mounted Police are involved, and said an investigation will be launched soon. The fire was still underway on the ground, he said, and the Canadian Armed Forces, the federal government, and provincial and local authorities were working together to mobilize support.

    CNN reported that firefighters were still working to keep the blaze from reaching residential areas. The Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire had crossed the base fence and was being actively managed, with firefighters from multiple jurisdictions responding. No evacuation order had been issued. Officials asked residents to stay away from the area, keep roads clear, and avoid the Wainwright Health Centre except for critical medical needs.

    Opposition leader Pierre Poilievre said he had spoken to McGuinty and Prime Minister Mark Carney about the fire, thanked emergency responders, and asked people to pray for Wainwright.

    A serious incident at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in east-central Alberta caused a major fire on Saturday, Oct. 17, 2026, Prime Minister Mark Carney said. The fire spread fast in high winds and crossed from near the base into the Municipal District of Wainwright No. 61, the district said. Emergency crews were working on the fire on Saturday evening, and no evacuation orders had been issued, Reuters reported.

    Carney posted on X at 8:05 p.m. Eastern time. "A serious incident creating a major fire has occurred at CFB Wainwright, in Alberta," he wrote. "Our thoughts are with all the brave women and men affected." Carney said Ottawa is working closely with the Alberta government and local authorities, and that he and Premier Danielle Smith are in close contact, as are their ministers and officials.

    About 10 minutes later, Smith posted on X that her government is learning of a significant and tragic fire at CFB Wainwright that is affecting many of Alberta's military personnel. She thanked the first responders and all those providing care and support to those affected. Defence Minister David McGuinty said on X at 8:54 p.m. Eastern time that he had been briefed by the Canadian Armed Forces on the major incident and that his thoughts are with the Armed Forces members in Wainwright.

    Fire crosses into the rural district

    In a Facebook post headed "INCIDENT UPDATE #001," the Municipal District of Wainwright No. 61 said: "A large fire originating near the Canadian Forces Base has spread rapidly due to high winds and has crossed into the Municipal District of Wainwright No. 61." Reuters reported that the municipality said the fire crossed the base fence into the nearby district earlier in the day and was being actively managed.

    The fire also shut a voting site. Elections Alberta said it had been informed of the large fire and had closed the advance voting location at the Wainwright Communiplex for Alberta's provincial referendum. Voters in Electoral Division 86 can still vote in advance at the Border Inn in Lloydminster or the Vermilion Elks Hall in Vermilion, the agency said. Referendum day is Monday, Oct. 19.

    What officials have not said

    National Defence told the Chatsworth Banner by email on Saturday evening: "We are aware of an incident in Wainwright, Alberta. The safety and security of our personnel, their families, and members of the surrounding community remain our highest priority." The department said it is closely monitoring the situation and will provide more information as it becomes available and can be confirmed.

    That statement did not give a cause, say whether anyone was injured, or name the units involved. As of Saturday evening, officials had not announced what caused the fire, how many people were hurt, or whether a training exercise was under way. Some reports have described an explosion. No official source has confirmed that. McGuinty later confirmed fatalities and injuries, but officials still have not given casualty numbers or a cause. No provincial emergency alert was active for the area as of about 10:05 p.m. Eastern time.

    CFB Wainwright, about 200 kilometres southeast of Edmonton, is a major Canadian Army training base used by units from across the country. Reuters called it one of the busiest Army bases in Canada. The Associated Press reported that the base is home to the Canadian Manoeuvre Training Centre, which conducts battlefield exercises, and the 3rd Canadian Division Training Centre. This story will be updated as officials release more information.
