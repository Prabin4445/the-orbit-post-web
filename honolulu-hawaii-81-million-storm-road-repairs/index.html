    Honolulu, Hawaii: $81.4 Million in Federal Money Heads to Hawaii to Repair Storm-Damaged Roads and Bridges

    The money from the U.S. Department of Transportation will pay to repair roads, bridges and other structures flooded in the Kona low storms that struck the islands in March 2026.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 24, 2026 · 1:35 UTC3 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Hawaii will receive nearly $81.4 million in federal money to repair roads, bridges and other structures that were flooded and damaged during the Kona low storms that hit the islands in March 2026.
    • "This new federal funding will help repair storm-damaged roads and structures as recovery from this year's historic flooding continues.
    • Schatz, a senior member of the Senate Appropriations Committee, said he had pressed for federal support for Hawaii's recovery and had spoken directly with U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy…

    Hawaii will receive nearly $81.4 million in federal money to repair roads, bridges and other structures that were flooded and damaged during the Kona low storms that hit the islands in March 2026. U.S. Sen. Brian Schatz announced the funding on October 22, 2026, and said it comes from the U.S. Department of Transportation.

    "This new federal funding will help repair storm-damaged roads and structures as recovery from this year's historic flooding continues. We're going to keep working to deliver federal resources so that affected communities can get back on their feet," Schatz said.

    Advertisement

    Schatz, a senior member of the Senate Appropriations Committee, said he had pressed for federal support for Hawaii's recovery and had spoken directly with U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy about the damage.

    The storms that did the damage

    Two Kona low storm systems struck Hawaii in March 2026. The first arrived around March 10 and soaked the islands. A second system followed during the week of March 15 to March 23, falling on ground that was already saturated. Governor Josh Green's emergency proclamations said the storms caused extensive damage to roads, highways, public infrastructure and buildings, and to homes and other private property, with catastrophic flooding in North Shore communities on Oahu.

    On March 20, 2026, the second storm produced life-threatening flash flooding on Oahu's North Shore. More than 13 inches of rain fell in parts of northern Oahu in about 12 hours, from the evening of March 19 into the morning of March 20. Flooding in the Haleiwa and Waialua areas cut off road access, prompted evacuation orders and triggered emergency operations. The state record of the response includes an Army helicopter crew flying over a washed out road on Oahu on March 21, 2026.

    Across the islands, the floods closed roads, entered homes and caused landslides. Green requested a federal disaster declaration on March 24, 2026, and it was approved on April 7, 2026. The new money is aimed at the network the storms broke: roads, bridges and other structures the state and counties must rebuild.

    Part of a national package

    The Hawaii money sits inside a national allocation the Department of Transportation announced on October 19, 2026. The department directed more than $1.6 billion in emergency relief to restore disaster-damaged roads and bridges in 34 states and several territories. In that release, Hawaii was listed at $81 million for damage from the Kona low storms in March 2026, the same funding Schatz detailed at $81.4 million.

    "Safe roads and bridges means rescue services arrive faster, medical professionals can reach those in need, and families can get the resources they need to recover," Duffy said in the department announcement.

    Hawaii will also receive a separate $2.3 million from the Federal Highway Administration's Emergency Relief Program to repair bridges on Kauai that were damaged by flooding in 2020, according to Schatz's announcement. That older damage shows in one line how long storm repairs can run in the islands: a flood from six years ago is still drawing repair money while the state works through the far larger March 2026 recovery.

    What it means for drivers

    For residents, the test of the funding will be visible on the road. Washed out sections, damaged bridges and flood-hit structures on Oahu and the neighbor islands are the routes people use for work, school, medical care and deliveries. State officials have not published a project-by-project list for the $81.4 million in the announcements reviewed for this story. The repair work will show up as state and county projects in the months ahead.
