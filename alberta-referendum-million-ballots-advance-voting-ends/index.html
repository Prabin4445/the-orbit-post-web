    Alberta Referendum Tops 1 Million Ballots Before Monday Vote

    Elections Alberta expects turnout of 80 to 85 percent. Advance polls close Saturday, Carney closed his tour before 5,000 people in Edmonton on Friday night, and referendum day is Monday.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 17, 2026 · 12:35 UTC · Updated October 18, 2026 12:40 UTC3 min read
    Share

    Key points

    • Updated 12:40 UTC, Oct 18, 2026.
    • Updated 18:35 UTC, Oct 17, 2026.
    • More than one million Albertans had already cast a ballot by Friday night, with one day of advance voting left before Monday's referendum on the province's future in Canada.

    Updated 12:40 UTC, Oct 18, 2026. Elections Alberta has set out how the result will arrive. Unofficial results will be published within 48 hours of voting places closing on Monday, October 19, and will be released live as counts are confirmed, with Question 10, the separation question, counted first, followed by Questions 1 to 9 in order. Official results will be announced on Thursday, October 29. Final polling before voting day points the same way as earlier surveys: Reuters reported that polls have consistently shown about 70 percent of Albertans want to remain in Canada, while roughly a quarter support independence.

    Updated 18:35 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Pro-Canada rallies and marches are being held across Alberta on Saturday, the last day of advance voting. The Alberta Federation of Labour said actions were planned in at least 15 towns and cities under its Fight Back Now campaign, including a downtown march and rally at the McDougall Centre in Calgary on Saturday and a Unity Rally at the Legislature in Edmonton on Sunday, October 18. AFL president Gil McGowan said: "We need to vote like our future depends on it, because it does." The federation says its campaign protests the referendum itself, which it calls manipulation, while also backing the wider pro-Canada effort led by groups such as Forever Canadian.

    Advertisement

    More than one million Albertans had already cast a ballot by Friday night, with one day of advance voting left before Monday's referendum on the province's future in Canada.

    Elections Alberta is expecting turnout of 80 to 85 percent of registered voters, the Edmonton Journal reported. Advance polls close on Saturday, October 17, after opening on October 13. On the first advance day alone, 174,532 people voted, more than the daily average in the 2023 provincial election.

    Carney makes his final pitch in Edmonton

    Prime Minister Mark Carney closed his Alberta tour on Friday night at the Edmonton Expo Centre, speaking to a Forever Canadian rally crowd of more than 5,000 people in the city where he grew up.

    “There are some outside our borders working to break us, so they can own us,” Carney said. He called this the worst time for a referendum and said it would be foolish to gamble with the province's future just to send a message.

    He did not dodge the energy fight. “The frustrations that many Albertans have felt are real,” he said, arguing that Alberta sits at the centre of his government's building plans and can help cut Canada's dependence on the United States.

    Forever Canadian leader Thomas Lukaszuk told the crowd their grandchildren would study this vote in school. Edmonton Mayor Andrew Knack urged voters to reject the other nine questions too, and the crowd answered with a chant of “no to nine.” Former premier Rachel Notley sent a recorded message, and hockey figure Kevin Lowe also spoke for staying in Canada.

    What is on the ballot

    Voters answer ten questions, each on its own ballot. Five deal with immigration, four with constitutional change, and the tenth asks whether Alberta should remain a province of Canada or start the legal process toward a binding referendum on separation. The separation question is not binding, and a vote to start the process does not take Alberta out of Canada.

    Premier Danielle Smith set the questions and has said she will vote to remain.

    The last days

    Advance polls run Saturday from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m., and voters can use any advance voting place inside their own electoral district. On Monday, October 19, polls run the same hours, but voters must use the place assigned to their home address. With ten ballots to count by hand, the count starts when polls close on Monday.

    Read the ten questions before you go. Elections Alberta says it cannot interpret the questions for voters, and lines have been long all week.
