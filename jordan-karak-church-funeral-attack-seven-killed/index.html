    Gunmen Kill Nine at Church Funeral in Karak, Jordan, as Wounded Toll Stays at 40

    Jordan's Public Security Directorate says another wounded person has died, raising the Karak church funeral attack toll to nine killed, with 40 wounded. CNN and AP also report nine dead, and a government spokesperson says the attackers held an extremist takfiri ideology.

    Update: Death toll rises to nine as another wounded person dies

    Updated 16:43 UTC, Oct. 24, 2026. Jordan's Public Security Directorate announced on Saturday that another person wounded in the Karak church attack has died from their injuries, raising the death toll to nine, the official Petra news agency reported. The new toll replaces the count of eight killed and 40 wounded that this article carried after the Reuters and CNN reports on Saturday morning. CNN has also revised its report to at least nine people killed and another 40 injured. The Associated Press, in its Saturday news summary, also reported nine dead and nearly 40 wounded, and described a survivor saying mourners crawled for their lives as gunmen fired inside the church during the funeral.

    Government spokesperson Mohammad Momani said on Saturday that the attackers subscribed to an extremist takfiri ideology, which brands people who reject a strict interpretation of Islam as apostates or nonbelievers, Fox News reported. Health Minister Ibrahim al-Budour said on Saturday that one critically injured patient remained hospitalised at Prince Ali Military Hospital in unstable condition, according to the same report. Jordanian authorities have not announced any arrests, and it remains unclear whether the gunmen were affiliated with any group.

    Update: Death toll rises to eight as security is stepped up across Jordan

    Updated 09:12 UTC, Oct. 24, 2026. Reuters reported on Saturday that gunmen killed eight people and wounded 40 in the church attack, as security was stepped up at malls, foreign embassies and other locations across Jordan, the United States embassy in Amman said. CNN also revised its report to at least eight people killed and another 40 injured. The new toll replaces the count of seven killed and 40 wounded that this article carried after the Associated Press report on Friday.

    Reuters said authorities were still searching for the attackers on Saturday, that there was no immediate claim of responsibility, and that it was too early to determine whether the attackers had links to known groups inside or outside Jordan. Reuters video showed police patrolling on Saturday outside the Hamoud church, about 120 km (75 miles) southwest of the capital Amman. A man at a hospital where the wounded were being treated, Tarek Ammarin, whose relative was injured, said: "A massacre, a massacre in every sense of the word," Reuters reported.

    Update: Wounded toll revised to 40 as AP matches the CNN count

    Updated 00:24 UTC, Oct. 24, 2026. The Associated Press reported on Friday that gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a church in Jordan, killing seven people and wounding 40 others, citing authorities. The figure matches the 40 injured that CNN reported in its latest update, a figure this article noted on Friday evening as unconfirmed by any other outlet at that time. The revised wounded count replaces the figure of 34 wounded that Jordan's director of public security gave at a news conference earlier on Friday, when he said 27 of the wounded were still in hospital.

    Jordan's Health Minister Ibrahim al-Budour said two of those wounded are in critical condition, CNN reported. The gunmen have not yet been captured, the public security agency said at a news conference on Friday afternoon, and officials described the shooting as a terrorist attack against Christians attending a funeral in the town of Hmoud.

    Update: Jordan declares three days of mourning as the king visits the wounded in Karak

    Updated 20:57 UTC, Oct. 23, 2026. Prime Minister Jafar Hassan declared three days of national mourning across Jordan starting on Friday, with flags flown at half mast on ministries, government departments and public institutions, the official Petra news agency reported. The prime minister's communique extended condolences to the families of the victims and to the people of Karak, and wished the injured a speedy recovery.

    Hassan said what happened in Karak was the result of extremist terrorist ideology rooted in takfir, the practice of declaring others to be infidels, and said Jordan would not allow society to go down that path, Petra reported, in remarks carried by CNN. King Abdullah travelled to Karak on Friday evening and visited the wounded at Prince Ali Bin Al Hussein Hospital, CNN reported, after earlier vowing that Jordan would confront the terrorist act with the harshest penalties.

    The United States Embassy in Jordan offered condolences to the victims and survivors and expressed solidarity with the king, CNN reported. The Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem and the Iraqi government also condemned the attack. The confirmed toll remained seven killed and 34 wounded, the count Jordan's director of public security gave at a news conference earlier on Friday. CNN reported 40 injured in its latest update, a figure no other outlet had confirmed by Friday evening, so this article keeps the director's count until officials revise it.

    Update: Police director puts the toll at seven killed and 34 wounded

    Updated 16:02 UTC, Oct. 23, 2026. Jordan's director of public security, Maj. Gen. Obeidallah Maaytah, told a news conference that seven people were killed and 34 wounded in the shooting at the Greek Orthodox church in Karak, the Associated Press reported. Maaytah said 27 of the wounded were still being treated in hospital. The toll replaces the directorate's earlier count of at least six killed and 28 injured, which is recorded below.

    A government statement said the attack was carried out by unknown assailants and that investigations were under way at full speed. The statement did not say whether any gunman had been captured or killed. State television images show two gunmen holding pistols approach the church and fire, then a third man holding a semiautomatic rifle open fire before all three run toward the church, AP reported.

    Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya, speaking at the same news conference in Karak, called the shooting a terrorist attack carried out by a group of extremists and said security services were working around the clock to reach the perpetrators. No named group had claimed the attack, and officials did not name the attackers.

    AP noted that Karak was also the scene of a deadly attack in 2016, when gunmen ambushed police in a series of attacks that included the Crusader castle in the city, killing 10 people, among them seven officers, and wounding dozens. Security forces killed those gunmen after a long standoff.

    Update: Officials revise the toll to six killed and 28 injured

    Updated 15:39 UTC, Oct. 23, 2026. Jordan's Public Security Directorate said at least six people were killed and 28 injured in the church shooting in Karak, CNN reported. State media had originally reported seven people killed. A spokesperson for the directorate confirmed at least six dead after Friday's attack on the Hamoud church, and Jordanian sources had earlier told Al Jazeera that seven people had been killed.

    King Abdullah described the shooting as a terrorist act and vowed a harsh response. "The blood of Jordanians is precious, and we will strike those who transgress against this country with an iron fist," the king said on X.

    CNN identified the church as St. George's Greek Orthodox Church in Hmood, a predominantly Christian town in northern Karak, about 90 miles south of Amman. Witness Tamer Halasa told Al-Mamlaka that more than 150 people were inside and outside the church, that one attacker was carrying an AK-47 rifle, and that the shooting continued for several minutes. Security camera footage circulating on social media showed three gunmen opening fire on the street before heading toward the church, CNN reported. The unidentified gunmen were still being pursued, the security agency said.

    At least seven people were killed and 10 others injured on Friday when gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a church in Jordan's Karak governorate, the state owned television station Al-Mamlaka reported.

    Two gunmen got out of a vehicle and opened fire during a funeral procession at the Hamoud church in Karak, the station said. Security forces deployed at the church and were pursuing the suspects, it reported.

    Fired on mourners for several minutes

    Khaled Burqan, a man who had been attending the funeral, told Al-Mamlaka that dozens of people had been attending the funeral. He said two men, one carrying a rifle and another carrying a pistol, opened fire on the mourners for several minutes.

    Reuters reported the same account, citing the station, in a first report published at 12:04 UTC on Friday. CNN also reported at least seven people killed in the attack, citing Jordanian state media. There was no immediate claim of responsibility, and Jordanian authorities did not say who was responsible for the shooting.

    The funeral and the first reports

    Al-Mamlaka, Jordan's state owned television station, was the first source of the news, and both Reuters and CNN built their first reports on its account. The station said the two gunmen got out of a vehicle and opened fire during the funeral procession at the Hamoud church. A funeral procession brings mourners out of the church and into the open, and the witness account describes the shooting lasting several minutes. Security forces deployed at the church and were pursuing the suspects, the station reported.

    A rare attack in Jordan

    Such attacks are extremely rare in Jordan, Reuters reported. The country is relatively stable, although Reuters said it has been increasingly targeted by Iranian attacks this year, since the United States and Israel launched strikes against Iran in February.

    Christians make up a minority of Jordan's population. The country is also home to several biblical sites that attract Christian pilgrims from around the world, which is one reason an attack on a church funeral drew attention well beyond Karak within hours of the first reports.

    What is not yet known

    The first reports did not name the people who were killed or injured, and officials had not said whether any suspect had been caught. Al-Mamlaka said security forces were pursuing the suspects after the shooting, and Reuters updated its report at about 13:06 UTC without a change to the toll of at least seven dead and 10 injured.

    No group had claimed the attack in the first reports, and authorities released no motive. The Orbit Post will update this article as Jordanian officials release the names of the victims and the result of the search for the gunmen.
