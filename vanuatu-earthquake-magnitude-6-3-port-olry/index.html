    Magnitude 6.3 Earthquake Strikes Off Vanuatu, No Tsunami Threat Reported

    The shallow quake struck about 200 kilometres from Port Olry at 04:20 UTC on Tuesday. The Pacific Tsunami Warning Centre said it posed no tsunami threat, and the first reports carried no word of damage or injuries.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 20, 2026 · 6:29 UTC2 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • A magnitude 6.3 earthquake struck in the sea off Vanuatu on Tuesday, October 20.
    • Reuters reported that the quake struck in waters about 200 kilometres from Port Olry, a village on the northeast coast of Espiritu Santo, the largest island in Vanuatu.
    • News Central reported that the Pacific Tsunami Warning Centre said the tremor posed no tsunami threat.

    A magnitude 6.3 earthquake struck in the sea off Vanuatu on Tuesday, October 20. The United States Geological Survey recorded the quake at 04:20 UTC, which was 15:20 local time in Vanuatu. The first reports carried no word of damage or injuries.

    Reuters reported that the quake struck in waters about 200 kilometres from Port Olry, a village on the northeast coast of Espiritu Santo, the largest island in Vanuatu. The USGS put the depth at 10 kilometres. That is shallow, and shallow quakes are felt more strongly than deeper ones of the same size because the energy is released closer to the surface. The epicentre sat far offshore, well away from the main towns on Espiritu Santo.

    Advertisement

    No tsunami threat reported

    News Central reported that the Pacific Tsunami Warning Centre said the tremor posed no tsunami threat. No warning was in force in the reports published in the first two hours after the quake. Some automated earthquake feeds flagged a tsunami field in their data for this event. A flag in a data feed is not a warning. The warning centre itself gave the all clear, according to the News Central report.

    By about 06:00 UTC, roughly 100 minutes after the quake, no Vanuatu government statement on damage had appeared in international reports. The offshore distance is one reason officials may have little to report: the epicentre was about 200 kilometres from the nearest village used as the reference point, and much farther from the capital, Port Vila.

    A restless stretch of sea

    The same patch of sea had already shaken twice in the hours before the main quake. A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck northwest of Port Olry at 18:52 UTC on Oct 19, 2026, at a depth of 10 kilometres. A magnitude 5.0 earthquake followed in the same area at 00:38 UTC on Oct 20, 2026, also at a depth of 10 kilometres. Seismologists often record clusters like this along the plate boundary that runs past Vanuatu, and a smaller quake before a larger one does not by itself predict what comes next.

    Vanuatu sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, where the Australian plate pushes under the Pacific plate. Earthquakes are a regular fact of life in the islands. A magnitude 6.3 earthquake struck near Norsup, on Malakula island, on Oct 8, 2026. The USGS issued a Green alert for that quake, meaning a low likelihood of casualties and damage, and officials reported no tsunami threat from it.

    The Orbit Post will update this story if Vanuatu officials report damage or injuries, or if the tsunami assessment changes.
