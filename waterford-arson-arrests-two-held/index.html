    Waterford Arson Attack: Two Arrested After Family of Four Killed

    Gardai arrested a man in his 20s and a woman in her 30s on Thursday afternoon over the firebombing of a Waterford home that killed Philip and Kim English and their sons Callum, 12, and Jayden, 9.

    Updated 18:35 UTC: Sixth person arrested over Waterford arson killings

    Gardai arrested a sixth person on Thursday afternoon, October 22, 2026, over the arson attack that killed Philip and Kim English and their sons Callum, 12, and Jayden, nine, at their home in the Birchwood area of Waterford city on October 13.

    The man, in his 20s, was arrested by Gardai in Waterford with help from the National Bureau of Criminal Investigation. He was held under Section 72 of the Criminal Justice Act 2006, as amended, at a Garda station in the south east. Gardai said further searches are expected as the investigation continues.

    Four people were being questioned over the fire on Thursday, and two people are already before the courts. On Tuesday, October 20, investigators arrested a man in his 50s and two women in their 30s. The total number of arrests in the case is now six.

    Sources

    The Irish Sun, reporting the Garda statement of October 22, 2026.

    Updated 15:46 UTC, Oct 17, 2026. Gardai have charged the man and the woman arrested over the Waterford arson attack, and both were due before a special sitting of Clonmel District Court at about 4.30 p.m. on Saturday. A Garda spokesperson said both people arrested in connection with the investigation have been charged. The pair, a man in his 20s and a woman in her 30s, were arrested on Thursday over the firebombing of the home at 7 Birch Avenue that killed Philip and Kim English and their sons Callum, 12, and Jayden, nine. Two other children remain in critical condition in hospital in Dublin. Garda Commissioner Justin Kelly said on Friday that officers had searched several properties and seized a number of items, and that he expects more arrests in the murder investigation. Philip, Kim, Callum and Jayden will be laid to rest together after a joint funeral Mass in Waterford on Monday.

    Gardai arrested a man and a woman on Thursday afternoon, October 15, in connection with the arson attack that killed a couple and two of their children in Waterford, hours after Ireland's parliament paid tribute to the family in Dublin.

    Philip English and his wife Kim died with their sons Callum, 12, and Jayden, nine, when their home at 7 Birch Avenue, in the Birchwood estate on the edge of Waterford city, was firebombed at about 5 a.m. on Tuesday, October 13. Two of the couple's other children, a girl of 14 and a boy of six, remain in critical condition in hospital in Dublin.

    The arrests

    A Garda spokesperson said investigating officers arrested a man aged in his 20s and a woman aged in her 30s for alleged offences contrary to section 72 of the Criminal Justice Act 2006, in connection with the arson attack and murder of four persons at 7 Birch Avenue. Section 72 covers offences linked to facilitating a criminal organisation. Both are detained under section 50 of the Criminal Justice Act 2007 at a Garda station in the Garda Eastern region.

    The Irish Sun reported that the woman is understood to have driven a car involved to the scene, while the man was involved at the scene. Gardai searched multiple properties during the week and seized a number of electronic devices and vehicles. Officers described the investigation as intense and very active. The Garda Technical Bureau is still examining the house, and post-mortem examinations on all four victims have been completed. The results are not being released for operational reasons. Family liaison officers are keeping the bereaved family updated.

    A debt the family did not owe

    Relatives say the house was targeted over someone else's drug debt of 20,000 euros. On Wednesday, October 14, a son of Mr English, John James English, told RTE News the family had been targeted for some time, were not involved in drugs and were innocent. He asked, in the Sun's account of his remarks, whether a debt of twenty thousand euros was worth nearly five lives.

    Jayden, the younger of the two boys who died, had a heart operation just two weeks ago, the Sun reported. Neighbours in Birchwood woke to screaming at about 5 a.m. on Tuesday and tried to get into the house to rescue the family, but the flames beat them back.

    Tributes in the Dail, and a funeral for four

    Hundreds of people attended a vigil in Birchwood on Wednesday, where balloons were released for the family. On Thursday, Sinn Fein TD David Cullinane raised the killings at Leaders' Questions in the Dail. Cullinane, who knows the family, said the attack showed "utter contempt for human life". He called for the Garda resources the murder investigation needs, including support from the Organised Crime Unit and the Criminal Assets Bureau. He also asked for urgent risk assessments, a named contact and safe emergency accommodation for families who report drug-related intimidation, and a review of how such threats are handled, with a report to parliament next week.

    The family has planned a funeral for all four. The death notices ask for family flowers only, with donations in lieu to Crumlin Children's Hospital in Dublin. Friends and teammates at Johnville FC, where Callum played, paid tribute to a boy his friends called the funniest in the group.

    What happens next

    The two people arrested can be questioned while they remain in detention, and gardai have repeated their appeal for information about the attack on Birch Avenue. The next steps are whether charges follow, the condition of the two injured children in Dublin, and the government's answer next week on how families threatened over drug debts are protected.
