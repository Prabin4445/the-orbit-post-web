    China Warns EU Over Kill Switch Plan to Bar Chinese Firms

    EU leaders backed work on a tool that could shut Chinese companies out of the single market. Beijing told Brussels on Friday not to expect pressure to win concessions.

    China warned the European Union on Friday not to expect a hard line to win trade concessions, hours after Brussels confirmed work on a new tool that could shut Chinese companies out of the single market fast.

    A foreign ministry spokesperson said the EU should not fantasise that pressure tactics would change China's position, according to comments carried by state media and reported by the South China Morning Post. The tool behind the row has a blunt nickname in Brussels: the kill switch.

    Advertisement

    What the kill switch would do

    EU leaders broadly backed exploring the instrument at a summit in Brussels on October 15, the South China Morning Post reported, citing people briefed on the meeting. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen presented the idea in her opening remarks, and the Commission's trade team could now start building it. Leaders asked the Commission to draw up a plan by the end of the year, Bloomberg reported via the Seoul Economic Daily.

    France and Germany proposed the mechanism. Lithuanian President Gitanas Nauseda put its logic plainly: if unfair practices are confirmed, measures that close the market completely are possible, calling it an extreme but very powerful tool.

    It would not stop at cars

    The flare-up comes a week after the two sides agreed to limit Chinese hybrid car shipments to Europe. That deal may not stay a one-off. The Commission has begun analysing trade data to see whether the hybrid cap, set at 350,000 units a year from 2027, can be extended to other sectors, the Seoul Economic Daily reported. Chemicals, machinery and wind turbines are named as candidates for safeguard quotas or tariffs. Von der Leyen said chemicals and machinery need swift protection.

    Why it matters

    This is the EU moving from case-by-case anti-subsidy probes to a standing weapon: prove distortion or coercion, and access to the world's richest single market can be switched off. For Beijing, the warning is about precedent. A tool built for cars today becomes a tool for chemicals, machinery and wind turbines tomorrow. For European firms that sell into China, the risk runs the other way: retaliation rarely stays in one sector.

    What happens next

    The Commission now drafts the rules, including what counts as an unfair practice serious enough to trigger the switch, and officials on both sides are expected to keep talking in the coming weeks. The argument is no longer about one car deal. It is about who writes the terms of access to the European market.
