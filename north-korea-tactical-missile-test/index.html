    North Korea Confirms Tactical Missile Test, Its 11th Launch of 2026

    KCNA said on Tuesday that Monday's launch tested an upgraded core element of a tactical ballistic missile. One missile flew more than 900 kilometers. It was North Korea's 11th launch of the year and its fourth in a month.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 13, 2026 · 10:27 UTC3 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • SEOUL: North Korea test fired a tactical ballistic missile on Monday, October 12, state media KCNA reported on Tuesday, October 13.
    • South Korea's military said the North fired multiple ballistic missiles toward the sea off its east coast.
    • Koyari said the launch was North Korea's 11th of the year, more than double its total for 2025.

    SEOUL: North Korea test fired a tactical ballistic missile on Monday, October 12, state media KCNA reported on Tuesday, October 13. KCNA said the test confirmed the successful technical upgrading of an important core element of the missile. It gave no further detail on what changed.

    South Korea's military said the North fired multiple ballistic missiles toward the sea off its east coast. The missiles, apparently of different types, lifted off at about 9:20 a.m. local time, 0020 GMT, from near Pyongyang. One type flew more than 900 kilometers, about 559 miles, the Joint Chiefs of Staff said. Japan's coast guard said two projectiles fell into the sea. Japan's vice minister of defense for policy, Takashi Koyari, said the second missile flew about 137 miles. He said Japan, South Korea and the United States were studying the longer flight, including the possibility of an irregular trajectory. No damage to ships or aircraft was reported.

    Koyari said the launch was North Korea's 11th of the year, more than double its total for 2025.

    A fast exchange of tests

    The launch was Pyongyang's fourth in a month. North Korea last fired a ballistic missile on October 3, when KCNA said it tested a hypersonic missile under the supervision of leader Kim Jong Un. This week's launch came days after South Korean President Lee Jae Myung oversaw his country's first test firing of a domestically developed hypersonic missile. A Joint Chiefs of Staff spokesperson called the North's launch a show of force in response to that test and a demonstration of missile capability.

    South Korea's National Security Office held an emergency meeting and condemned the launches as violations of United Nations Security Council resolutions. North Korea has been under UN sanctions since 2006, aimed at halting its nuclear weapons and ballistic missile programs.

    On Sunday, Kim Jong Un visited a defense industry museum that displays weapons developed under his leadership. KCNA reported that he said the country's weaponry would be developed in a more strenuous manner.

    Trump talks of a meeting

    The launch landed in the middle of a diplomatic opening. President Donald Trump told reporters on Monday that he may meet Kim during a coming visit to Asia. Trump has committed to meeting the North Korean leader before the end of 2026. The two sides now hold a familiar pattern. Missiles fly, statements condemn, and talk of a summit continues in the same week.

    Why it matters

    Each test gives North Korea data. A tactical missile that flies 900 kilometers covers all of South Korea and much of Japan. An upgraded core element, if the claim holds, points to work on reliability rather than range. That kind of progress is slow, technical and hard to sanction away. It is also the kind that matters most if the missiles are ever used.

    What happens next

    Seoul, Tokyo and Washington will publish their own readings of the flight in the coming days. Watch for whether South Korea answers with another test of its own, and whether Trump's Asia trip produces a date for a meeting with Kim.

    3. Oct 13, 2026KCNA says the Monday test was a tactical ballistic missile and confirmed an upgrade to a core element of the missile.
