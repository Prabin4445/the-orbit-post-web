    Stocks Slide, Gold Holds Above $4,100 as Hot Inflation Reshapes Rate Bets

    Hot producer prices and strong retail sales pushed the Nasdaq 100 down 3.28 percent, sent the Nikkei down 4.9 percent, and kept gold above $4,100 as traders priced in higher rates for longer.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 15, 2026 · 14:35 UTC · Updated October 15, 2026 22:38 UTC7 min read
    Share
    Read inENESNEHIFR

    Key points

    • Silver ended Thursday higher, while the fund that tracks the Nasdaq 100 ended lower.
    • Reuters, in its Trading Day wrap, put silver up about 3 percent on the day, with the dollar down for its biggest fall in six weeks and the 10 year Treasury yield down 10 basis points to 5.08 percent.
    • Nvidia fell to $217.23, down 4.94 percent from $228.52 in the markets watcher reading late on Thursday.

    Updated 22:38 UTC

    Silver ended Thursday higher, while the fund that tracks the Nasdaq 100 ended lower. Silver rose 3.54 percent to $57.515 an ounce, from $55.55. QQQ fell 2.09 percent to $728.00, from $743.57. The fund trimmed the 2.49 percent loss in the 15:14 UTC reading in this report, while silver rose past the $57.31 reading at that hour.

    Reuters, in its Trading Day wrap, put silver up about 3 percent on the day, with the dollar down for its biggest fall in six weeks and the 10 year Treasury yield down 10 basis points to 5.08 percent. The Wall Street Journal said front month Comex silver settled 2.32 percent higher at $56.837 an ounce and gold futures rose 1.54 percent to $4,147.70, as yields and the dollar fell. Lower yields helped metals. Tech stayed under the rate pressure set out below, and Reuters said the selloff in AI stocks deepened on fears that higher interest rates will make it harder to fund AI investment.

    Updated 19:54 UTC

    Nvidia fell to $217.23, down 4.94 percent from $228.52 in the markets watcher reading late on Thursday. Barron's put the shares at $217.25 on Thursday and said the drop added to a 3.2 percent loss on Wednesday, as fears over AI spending kept pressure on chip stocks.

    The worry runs through CoreWeave, the debt heavy data centre operator tied closely to Nvidia as investor and supplier. Reuters said the AI selloff deepened on fears that higher interest rates will make AI investment harder to fund, after hot inflation data lifted bets on Federal Reserve rate hikes. Barron's reported that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, speaking at an event in Sweden, said CoreWeave does not need a backstop and that its cost of capital is decreasing. Traders still sold first. If CoreWeave and other smaller cloud firms struggle to borrow, orders for chips and networking gear could slow, and Nvidia sits at the centre of that order book.

    Updated 15:14 UTC

    Silver jumped while tech shares kept falling. Silver rose 3.17 percent to $57.31 an ounce, from $55.55. QQQ, the fund that tracks the Nasdaq 100, fell 2.49 percent to $725.09, from $743.57.

    Reuters said gold and silver rebounded as the dollar eased after an 18 month high and Treasury yields retreated. Spot silver had already gained 1.1 percent to $56.26 earlier in the day, Reuters reported. CNBC TV18 also reported gold and silver rising on Thursday as investors weighed the rate outlook after hot inflation data.

    Tech moved the other way for the same rate reason. Reuters said the selloff in AI stocks deepened on fears that higher interest rates will make it harder to fund AI investment. TSMC struck a cautious note for 2027 after reporting a record quarterly profit, and CoreWeave and Oracle stayed under pressure after heavy falls on Wednesday. CNN reported the same drivers on Thursday, and pointed to slower revenue growth at Anthropic and tougher competition from China. A hot producer price reading added pressure. Producer prices rose 1.3 percent in September and 6.3 percent from a year earlier, Reuters reported.

    Stocks fell hard on Wall Street on Thursday, October 15, while gold held above $4,100 an ounce, after two days of inflation data came in hotter than economists expected and forced traders to rethink how soon the Federal Reserve might cut interest rates.

    By about 14:05 UTC, the Dow Jones Industrial Average stood at 51,869.76, up 0.39 percent. The broader S and P 500 index was at 7,696.04, down 1.4 percent. The Nasdaq 100, heavy with technology shares, took the worst of the selling, at 29,566.20, down 3.28 percent.

    The pressure built in Asia before New York even opened. Japan's Nikkei 225 closed down 4.9 percent at 64,425.95, its worst day in months. India moved the other way. The Sensex closed up 2.11 percent at 74,315.61, and the Nifty 50 rose 1.66 percent to 22,997.35. In London, the FTSE 100 rose about 1.4 to 1.5 percent to roughly 10,801 to 10,814, reaching a near one-month high. Reuters reported that banks led the London market higher after a pledge on fiscal discipline, and official figures showed the British economy grew 0.1 percent in August.

    Why it matters

    Two inflation reports in two days changed the mood. On October 14, the consumer price index showed prices rose 3.7 percent in the year to September. The Nasdaq fell nearly 2 percent that day. Then on October 15, producer prices, which track what businesses pay for goods before they reach shop shelves, rose 1.3 percent in September alone. That was the biggest monthly rise since March 2022, and it put producer prices 6.3 percent higher than a year earlier.

    At the same time, retail sales rose 0.8 percent, well above the 0.3 percent forecast. Shoppers are still spending. A strong shopper plus rising prices is a hard mix for the Federal Reserve. It gives the central bank little reason to cut rates soon, and it raises the question traders had pushed aside: whether the next move could even be a rise.

    Gold priced that fear directly. Gold futures rose 1.4 percent to $4,165 an ounce in early trading covered by Investopedia, and the metal held above $4,100 through the day.

    The context

    The technology selloff deepened through the session. Nvidia fell 6.2 percent to $214.35, and Tesla fell 4.13 percent to $359.75 on October 15. Orbit Post has covered both falls in separate market flashes today. Chips and electric car shares had carried much of this year's gains, built on heavy spending on artificial intelligence. When borrowing costs look set to stay high, those future profits are worth less today, and the shares fall fastest.

    Crypto felt the same pull. Bitcoin traded at about $83,246, down from above $84,000 the day before. Investopedia's morning note put bitcoin at $80,800 early in the day, so buyers did step in, but the recovery stayed fragile. Ethereum traded at about $2,502, and Solana at about $107.

    Bond markets set the price underneath all of this. The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note stood at about 5.09 percent on the morning of October 15, per Investopedia. That yield is the benchmark that mortgage rates and many loans follow.

    • Consumer prices: up 3.7 percent year on year in September

    • 10-year Treasury yield: about 5.09 percent

    What this means for ordinary money

    For savers, a higher-for-longer rate path is not all bad. Savings accounts and money market funds keep paying solid interest while the Fed holds back. For borrowers, the news is worse. A 10-year Treasury yield near 5.09 percent keeps mortgage rates high, so anyone waiting for cheaper home loans to buy a house, or to refinance, may wait longer.

    For retirement accounts, days like Thursday hurt on paper. A 401k heavy in technology funds would have fallen more than one balanced across industries, which is why the Dow rose while the Nasdaq 100 dropped 3.28 percent. The bigger risk for households is not one session. It is inflation staying near 3.7 percent month after month, quietly shrinking what a paycheck buys.

    What happens next

    Traders now wait for the next Federal Reserve meeting and for any official to signal whether a rate rise is truly on the table. Company earnings over the next two weeks will also be read differently: investors will ask whether firms can pass higher producer costs to customers without losing them. If the next inflation report cools, Thursday may look like a scare. If it runs hot again, gold above $4,100 and a 5 percent Treasury yield may become the new normal.
