    FBI Arrests OpenAI Engineer Accused of Bugging Conference Room

    Prosecutors say Abdulrahman Alfozan hid an encrypted recorder behind a TV in the room used for OpenAI's most sensitive meetings. He faces up to five years in prison.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 14, 2026 · 7:36 UTC · Updated October 20, 2026 3:05 UTC4 min read

    Updated 03:05 UTC, Oct 20, 2026

    New court filings in the case say Alfozan was in frequent contact with a member of the Saudi royal family who was recruiting him for an artificial intelligence initiative in Saudi Arabia, and that his wife and young son left the United States a day after his arrest on tickets bought by a Saudi military official. Prosecutors unsealed an 11-page detention filing on Sunday, October 18, arguing that Alfozan should stay in custody. Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse denied bail, according to reports of the October 19 detention hearing.

    According to the filing, the royal family member messaged Alfozan on WhatsApp as recently as October 5, 2026, and told him in May 2026, "Saudi needs you more then the US", spelling as written, as part of the effort to recruit him. Alfozan replied "100", prosecutors say, and told the royal he would not leave OpenAI until his stock vested. He was arrested on October 9, three days before a planned one-way flight from San Francisco to Riyadh by way of Istanbul with his wife and son. His wife, Lujain Alassaf, and their three-month-old son flew to Doha, Qatar, on October 10 on business-class tickets paid for by a member of the Saudi military, the filing says.

    Prosecutors also say investigators found signs Alfozan may have planned to install more recording devices, and they call him a danger to national security and a serious flight risk. His lawyer, William Pernik, argued that Alfozan's nationality is not evidence of flight, said the planned travel was for family and immigration reasons, and asked for release on home detention with GPS monitoring. The filing does not allege that Saudi Arabia directed the alleged recording. OpenAI has said there is no indication Alfozan accessed or took model weights, source code or customer data. The investigation is continuing and prosecutors say more charges are possible.

    Federal prosecutors say an OpenAI engineer hid an encrypted listening device behind a television in the room where the company's top executives hold their most sensitive meetings, then kept coming back to check on it.

    Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national who worked on OpenAI's product team, was arrested by the FBI on October 9 and appeared in federal court in San Francisco on Tuesday. The Justice Department unsealed the case the same day. Alfozan is charged with using, or trying to use, an electronic device to intercept oral communications. If convicted, he faces up to five years in prison and a $250,000 fine.

    A device wrapped in toilet paper

    According to the criminal complaint, OpenAI employees found the device on September 30, plugged into a USB port on the back of a television in an executive conference room. It was wrapped in toilet paper. Prosecutors say it had been installed around September 28 and that its contents were encrypted.

    The room is used for what prosecutors called highly confidential discussions about proprietary research, product plans and business opportunities. OpenAI said the device had advanced encryption, audio monitoring and a way to move data out that was designed to avoid detection.

    OpenAI caught the case with its own tools. "We used our models to understand the device and where it had come from, identifying that a specific employee had planted it a few days earlier," the company said in a statement. "We referred the matter to the FBI."

    Caught on camera, more than once

    Alfozan joined OpenAI in July 2025 and, according to the complaint, had no authorization to be in or near the executive conference room. Security footage still showed him near it on nearly a dozen occasions in September. OpenAI then installed a camera in the room itself. Last week, prosecutors say, that camera caught him walking in and checking behind the television where the device had been hidden.

    The complaint also says Alfozan used an internal tool to look up company building addresses, meeting rooms, room capacities, calendars and the locations of executive staff. Days before his arrest, OpenAI staff opened an unattended bag he had left in a different room and found his wallet, identification and a second recording device similar to the first one.

    Prosecutors say a chatbot account linked to Alfozan's spouse, which shared identifiers with his own account, had been used to ask about building and hiding recording equipment, including continuous audio recorders with Wi-Fi and live listening.

    Washington treats it as a security case

    FBI Director Kash Patel said the device was meant to capture sensitive communications about the company's superintelligence technology, and called protecting that work one of the bureau's highest counterintelligence priorities. Attorney General Todd Blanche said theft of intellectual property involving cutting-edge superintelligence technology is a serious national security threat.

    OpenAI says the damage appears contained. The company said it has no indication that Alfozan accessed or stole model weights, source code or customer data. He was fired before his arrest.

    What happens next

    Alfozan remains in federal custody. A detention hearing is set for Monday, October 19, in the Northern District of California. The FBI says its investigation is continuing, and prosecutors have laid out evidence they say points to plans for more listening devices in other sensitive locations. It was not immediately clear whether Alfozan has a lawyer who can speak for him.
