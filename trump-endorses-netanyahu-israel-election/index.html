    Trump Endorses Netanyahu for Reelection Ahead of Israel's October 27 Vote

    President Donald Trump gave Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu his complete and total endorsement on Sunday in a Truth Social post, ending months in which he had withheld it. Israel votes on October 27 in its first national election since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack.

    Key points

    • President Donald Trump endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for reelection on Sunday, giving him a boost he had withheld for months before Israel votes on October 27.
    • Trump called Netanyahu a great warrior prime minister and an even greater partner for peace in combatting the Islamic Republic of Iran, the Associated Press reported.
    • Trump closed the post by saying it should serve as his complete and total endorsement of Netanyahu in his race for reelection as prime minister of Israel.

    President Donald Trump endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for reelection on Sunday, giving him a boost he had withheld for months before Israel votes on October 27. Trump announced the endorsement in a post on Truth Social, nine days before the election.

    Trump called Netanyahu a great warrior prime minister and an even greater partner for peace in combatting the Islamic Republic of Iran, the Associated Press reported. In the same post, quoted by Reuters, he wrote that Netanyahu has been strong, brave and brilliant in the face of tremendous opposition, including opposition coming from Israel itself.

    Advertisement

    Trump closed the post by saying it should serve as his complete and total endorsement of Netanyahu in his race for reelection as prime minister of Israel. He added that Netanyahu will never let Israel down.

    An endorsement withheld for months

    The endorsement matters in part because it did not come sooner. The Associated Press said Trump had withheld it for months while the two leaders' strategies in the Middle East diverged. Its absence had been a setback for Netanyahu, a Likud politician who has presented his close ties to Trump as a reason for voters to keep him in power.

    Trump made clear in the post that he saw the choice in Israel as a choice about Iran. If Israel had the wrong leader, which he said would be anyone other than Netanyahu, the United States would have to do that work itself, and he said he was not inclined to do so, the New York Post reported in its account of the full post.

    First election since the October 7 attack

    Israelis vote on October 27 in the first national election since the Hamas attack on October 7, 2023. That attack shook the country and pushed the Middle East into turmoil, as Israel went to war in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran, Reuters reported.

    The vote will decide whether Netanyahu stays in power after one of the most consequential periods in Israel's history, or whether centrist rivals defeat him on a promise to open a new era. Netanyahu's party faces a tough race, and Trump's popularity in Israel makes his backing a prize for a campaign that has stressed the Washington relationship.

    With just over a week of campaigning left, the endorsement puts the United States president openly inside Israel's election argument. Netanyahu can now point to a public statement from Trump, not only to past meetings, when he tells voters that he is the partner Washington wants. His rivals will have to answer that claim in the final days before October 27.
