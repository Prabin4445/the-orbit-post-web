    US Halts Obamacare Subsidies for 189,000 Duplicate Enrollees

    CMS halted subsidies for 189,000 people who held marketplace coverage alongside Medicare, Medicaid or CHIP, or who were listed as deceased, in a move officials value at 738 million dollars this year.

    The federal government has halted Obamacare subsidies for 189,000 people who held subsidized marketplace coverage while also holding Medicare, Medicaid or CHIP coverage, or who were listed as deceased.

    The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services identified the 189,000 people in 2026. Each person held subsidized coverage through the Federal Marketplace, HealthCare.gov, and also held another form of coverage, or had died. The Department of Health and Human Services did not break the total down by category.

    Advertisement

    Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced the action at a press conference in Washington on October 14. The administration estimates the move prevents 738 million dollars in improper subsidy payments this year, and more than 1.5 billion dollars on an annualized basis.

    Notices first, then removal

    People flagged as duplicate enrolled will not lose subsidies at once. They are notified and given 30 days to provide evidence about their coverage. Estates of people listed as deceased also get 30 days.

    The government expects to prevent more than one million months of improperly subsidized coverage by the end of the year. That figure counts each month of duplicate coverage stopped, across all the people affected.

    Officials said most affected people do not lose insurance. They typically keep Medicare, Medicaid or CHIP, the coverage they already held alongside the marketplace plan. The subsidy ends, not the person's main coverage.

    Removals already made this year

    CMS Deputy Administrator Peter Nelson said removals already made this year cover 58,000 people who already had Medicare. Another 11,000 subsidized plans were ended for people who had died.

    CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz said Wednesday's action is separate from last month's steps. He claimed nearly six billion dollars saved overall from the wider enforcement effort.

    That wider effort has run in stages. In September, Vice President JD Vance said enrollment was halted for more than 760,000 people believed to have been fraudulently signed up, an estimated saving of 2.2 billion dollars.

    Duplicate enrollment enforcement resumed in 2025. That earlier work ended subsidies for about 551,000 enrollees who were already covered by Medicaid.

    Why it matters

    Subsidies are the core of the marketplace system. They lower monthly premiums for people who buy coverage on HealthCare.gov. Paying them for people who already hold public coverage, or who have died, wastes public money and distorts enrollment counts.

    The test for the administration is accuracy. A person wrongly flagged as duplicate enrolled faces a deadline and a paperwork burden to keep support. The 30 day window is the safeguard the government points to. Advocates and state officials will watch how many notices turn into disputes, and how many people prove their marketplace coverage was correct.

    What happens next is a paperwork race through the end of the year. CMS must process responses, end subsidies where the duplicate record stands, and keep coverage in place where people show they were flagged in error. The savings figures are estimates. The final count depends on how those cases resolve.

    Key numbers

    • People affected by the October action: 189,000
    • Estimated improper payments prevented this year: 738 million dollars
    • Annualized estimate: more than 1.5 billion dollars
    • Improperly subsidized months to be prevented by year end: more than one million
    • Time to provide evidence after notice: 30 days
    • Medicare duplicates already removed this year: 58,000
    • Plans ended for deceased people this year: 11,000

    Timeline

    1. 2025Duplicate enrollment enforcement resumes and ends subsidies for about 551,000 enrollees already covered by Medicaid.
    2. Sep 2026Vice President JD Vance says enrollment was halted for more than 760,000 people believed fraudulently signed up.
    3. Oct 14, 2026Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and CMS announce subsidies halted for 189,000 duplicate or deceased enrollees.
