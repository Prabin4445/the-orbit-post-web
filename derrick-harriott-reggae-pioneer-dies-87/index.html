    Derrick Harriott, Jamaican Singer and Reggae Producer of The Loser, Dies at 87

    Harriott co-founded The Jiving Juniors in 1958, founded the Crystal label in 1962, produced Dennis Brown and Big Youth, and recorded one of the earliest dub albums. He died at home in Jamaica on October 24, aged 87.

    Derrick Harriott, the Jamaican singer and record producer who helped shape ska, rocksteady and reggae, died at home on Saturday, October 24, 2026, shortly after 1:00 a.m. local time. He was 87. Dorrel Mattocks, who operates the Derrick Harriott Music Store in St Andrew, confirmed the death to The Gleaner and said Harriott had recently been discharged from hospital after a period of illness. Clash Magazine reported that the passing was confirmed on Saturday through multiple sources, and that The Jamaica Observer reported he died peacefully at home.

    Mattocks told The Gleaner: "He was sick and was in and out of hospital."

    A career that started in the late 1950s

    Harriott was born in Kingston, Jamaica, on February 6, 1939. He began his musical career in the late 1950s and co-founded the vocal group The Jiving Juniors in 1958. The group recorded early hits that included Lollipop Girl and Over the River, and the work placed Harriott inside Jamaican popular music at the moment when local recording was taking shape as an industry of its own.

    In 1962 he started his solo career and established the Crystal record label. His solo recordings included I Care and his signature hit The Loser. He also recorded Solomon, Walk The Streets, What Can I Do and I am Only Human. The Loser remained the song most closely tied to his name as a singer, while Crystal gave him a base from which to record other artists and to control how his own work reached the public.

    The producer behind other voices

    As a producer, Harriott worked with Dennis Brown, Big Youth, The Ethiopians, Keith and Tex and The Kingstonians. His production work contributed to the development of early reggae and dub, and it included recordings made at King Tubby's studio. Producers in that period shaped the sound of a record as directly as singers did, because they chose the artists, the musicians and the studio treatment that listeners heard.

    His studio band, the Crystallites, recorded the instrumental album Undertaker in 1970. Swing magazine named Harriott top producer of 1970. Scrub A Dub, released in 1974 and mixed at King Tubby's studio, was one of the earliest dub albums. Dub took finished recordings and rebuilt them around rhythm, echo and space, and Harriott was working in that form while it was still new.

    Honours late in life

    The Jamaican government awarded Harriott the Order of Distinction in 2009, in recognition of his contribution to Jamaican music. The Jamaica Reggae Industry Association, known as JaRIA, gave him a Lifetime Achievement Award in 2019. Both honours came decades after the records that built his reputation, and both pointed to the same body of work: songs he sang, records he produced and a label he ran from Kingston.

    Harriott's death closes a career that ran from the vocal group era of the late 1950s through ska and rocksteady and into reggae and dub. The artists he produced, including Dennis Brown and Big Youth, carried Jamaican music to audiences far beyond the island, and the recordings he made with the Crystallites remain part of the record of how dub began.
