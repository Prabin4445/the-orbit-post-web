    Stolen SUV Hits Woman, Nearly Plows Into Melbourne Marathon

    A 16-year-old allegedly drove a stolen Ford Escape into a pedestrian and within meters of the cordoned Melbourne Marathon course on Sunday morning. Victoria Police say it was not terrorism.

    OPBy Orbit PostOctober 11, 2026 · 14:30 UTC3 min read
    Share

    Key points

    • A 16-year-old boy from the Dandenong area allegedly drove a stolen white Ford Escape through central Melbourne on Sunday morning, struck a pedestrian near the Melbourne Marathon route, and drove…
    • Around 10:45 a.m. local time, the SUV hit a woman in her 50s from Southbank at the corner of Elizabeth and Flinders streets, outside Flinders Street Station.
    • Police say the driver then made a U-turn, struck parked cars and signage, and approached the blocked-off marathon course.

    A 16-year-old boy from the Dandenong area allegedly drove a stolen white Ford Escape through central Melbourne on Sunday morning, struck a pedestrian near the Melbourne Marathon route, and drove within meters of the cordoned course before fleeing, Victoria Police said. The incident is not being treated as terrorism or a targeted attack, police said, and that point is stated plainly: there is no evidence of either.

    Around 10:45 a.m. local time, the SUV hit a woman in her 50s from Southbank at the corner of Elizabeth and Flinders streets, outside Flinders Street Station. She was taken to Royal Melbourne Hospital with non-life-threatening upper-body injuries, according to Ambulance Victoria. Bystanders pulled her off the road so she would not be hit again.

    Advertisement

    Police say the driver then made a U-turn, struck parked cars and signage, and approached the blocked-off marathon course. A traffic-control truck positioned itself at the course intersection and blocked his path. The teenager fled, and was later arrested in Albert Park. He is expected to be interviewed. The vehicle had been stolen from a business in Cranbourne South around 2 a.m. Sunday.

    The marathon continued. Organizers diverted runners around the affected intersection. The field numbered more than 60,000 participants, according to race reports.

    Why it matters

    A mass-participation marathon is one of the most exposed events a city can stage. Tens of thousands of runners are strung out over 42 kilometers of public roads, and the course depends on hard closures, physical barricades, and traffic marshals at every intersection. Sunday's incident is a stress test of that system: a stolen car driven erratically through the central business district reached a course intersection while runners were on the road.

    The near miss is the story, not just the collision. What stopped the car from entering the runner pack was a traffic-control truck parked across the intersection, not the perimeter cordon itself. That will be the question investigators and race organizers answer in the coming days: how a vehicle crossed the inner security layers before being blocked.

    The context

    Major marathons have hardened their courses over the past decade. London, Berlin, and New York close roads with concrete barriers and steel truck blockers at key crossings, with police stationed at every junction. Melbourne's marathon uses the standard playbook: a fully closed course, traffic marshals, and police support. But no major road race can seal every meter of 42 kilometers, and the outer rings of a city course always sit next to open traffic.

    The driver, police say, was a 16-year-old allegedly driving a car stolen hours earlier. Under Victoria's juvenile justice system, teen car theft is handled in the youth justice stream, with detention available for serious offenses. That is a separate debate from Sunday's immediate one, and it should not be confused with it: the police position is that this was dangerous driving, not an attack.

    Melbourne's emergency services moved fast. Ambulance Victoria treated the injured woman at the scene and took her to Royal Melbourne. The marathon resumed without further disruption to the field.

    Key numbers

    • 16: age of the alleged driver, from the Dandenong area
    • 50s: age of the injured woman, from Southbank
    • 10:45 a.m.: local time the woman was struck at Elizabeth and Flinders streets
    • 2 a.m.: approximate time the Ford Escape was stolen in Cranbourne South
    • 60,000+: marathon participants, per race reports

    What happens next

    The teenager will be interviewed, and police will say whether charges follow. Expect Melbourne's event organizers and the state government to review how the vehicle reached the course intersection, and whether the barrier plan for next year's race changes. Similar reviews have followed near misses at other major races; the usual outcome is more heavy vehicles parked at vulnerable crossings and earlier, wider road closures.
